# UCLA Loneliness Scale

## Description

Psychometric self-administered scale to assess the subjective perception of loneliness, including feelings of emptiness, lack of companionship, interpersonal disconnection, and absence of affective support.

## Origin and development

Developed by Russell (1996). The short version of 6 items (ULS-6) was adapted by Neto (2014). Adapted to the work domain by Andel et al. (2021) to measure weekly work loneliness.

## What it measures

- Perceived loneliness (subjective)
    
- Feelings of isolation and disconnection
    
- Lack of companionship and affective support
    
- In the work-adapted version: loneliness in the occupational context
    

## How it is applied

Likert scale of 5 points (1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree). The short version has 6 items; the full version has 20 items. Can be applied weekly to capture intra-individual variations.

## How it was used in the studies

- [[Alroomi and Mohamed (2022)]]: Short version to assess subjective loneliness among isolated oil and gas workers.
    
- [[Andel et al. (2021)]]: ULS-6 adapted to the work domain and applied weekly.
    

## Advantages and limitations

**Advantages:** Brief, validated, captures the subjective dimension of isolation, allows longitudinal application.

**Limitations:** Does not distinguish social loneliness from emotional loneliness; may overlap with depressive symptoms; requires adaptation to specific contexts.
