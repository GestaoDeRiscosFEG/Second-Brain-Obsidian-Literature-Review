# VAS

## Description

Visual analogue scale, usually represented by a 100 mm horizontal line on which the respondent marks a point between two extremes (for example, "no pain" and "worst imaginable pain"). Allows quantification of subjective sensations simply and quickly.

## Origin and development

Used for decades in clinical and occupational research. The versions for pain, stress, and comfort were validated by different authors (Lesage et al., 2012; Yang et al., 2019; Smith, 2000). Can be adapted to different contexts and dimensions.

## What it measures

- Pain intensity (0 = no pain, 10 = worst imaginable pain)
    
- Perceived stress (0 = none, 10 = extreme)
    
- Environmental comfort: lighting, temperature, noise
    
- Workstation comfort: chair, desk, monitor, keyboard
    
- Other subjective dimensions (fatigue, anxiety, well-being)
    

## How it is applied

Scale of 100 mm or 0 to 10 points. The respondent marks a point on the line or chooses a number. Can be applied in person or online (numeric scale). Number of items varies by application: not reported.

- [[Ekpanyaskul and Padungtod (2021)]]: 10-point VAS of occupational stress during telework.
    
- [[Paluszak et al. (2025)]]: VAS for Pain in a randomized controlled trial protocol.
    

## Advantages and limitations

**Advantages:** Simple, fast, low cost, sensitive to change, applicable to several dimensions.

**Limitations:** Subjective; requires careful interpretation; may vary between respondents in how they use the scale.
