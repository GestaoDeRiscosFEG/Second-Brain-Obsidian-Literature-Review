# COM-B Model

## Description

Theoretical framework for understanding behavior and identifying intervention targets. Postulates that behavior (B) results from the interaction between Capability (C), Opportunity (O), and Motivation (M). Widely used in public health and occupational health to design behavior change interventions.

## Origin and development

Not reported in the extraction.

## What it measures

- Capability: physical and psychological (knowledge, skills)
    
- Opportunity: physical and social (environment, social norms)
    
- Motivation: automatic and reflective (habits, beliefs, intentions)
    
- Behaviour: the target behavior to be modified
    

## How it is applied

Used as a theoretical framework for developing interview and focus group guides and for thematic categorization of data. Also guides the design of interventions (e.g., education for capability, environmental changes for opportunity, incentives for motivation).

## Advantages and limitations

**Advantages:** Solid theoretical basis, applicable to various behaviors, guides practical interventions, allows systematic analysis.

**Limitations:** Does not capture all social and structural determinants; requires adaptation to specific contexts; may simplify the complexity of behavior.
