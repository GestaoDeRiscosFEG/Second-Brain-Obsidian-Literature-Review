# EWA

## Description

Work analysis methodology that combines observation, interviews, and document analysis to understand the real activity of workers and identify risks and opportunities for improvement. Structured in 5 phases: demand, company/population, prescribed tasks, activity observations, and diagnosis/intervention.

## Origin and development

Developed by Guérin et al. (2007) in the Franco-Belgian ergonomics tradition. Used by Faraco et al. (2025) to analyze support teams in the oil and gas industry.

## What it measures

- Demand: understanding of the problem and objectives of the analysis
    
- Company/population: characterization of the organizational context and workers
    
- Prescribed tasks: what is formally required of workers
    
- Activity observations: what workers actually do
    
- Diagnosis/intervention: identification of problems and proposal of improvements
    

## How it is applied

Combines direct observation, interviews, document analysis, and, in some cases, filming. Can be adapted to remote contexts (online interviews, analysis of digital communications).

## How it was used in the studies

- [[Faraco et al. (2025)]]: Used to analyze the impact of the pandemic on support teams in the oil and gas industry, guiding the understanding of the problem and the data analysis.
    

## Advantages and limitations

**Advantages:** Holistic approach, captures the complexity of real work, guides contextual interventions.

**Limitations:** Requires time and resources; may be difficult to apply in remote contexts; depends on the analyst's expertise.
