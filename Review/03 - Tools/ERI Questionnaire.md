# ERI Questionnaire

## Description

Questionnaire based on Siegrist's Effort-Reward Imbalance Model, which postulates that chronic work stress arises when the effort invested is not compensated by adequate rewards (salary, recognition, job security, career opportunities).

## Origin and development

Developed by Siegrist et al. (2004). The French version was validated by Niedhammer et al. (2000b). One of the most used theoretical models in occupational health, alongside Karasek's Demand-Control Model.

## What it measures

- Extrinsic effort: demands and obligations of work
    
- Reward: money, esteem, job security, promotion opportunities
    
- Overcommitment: personal attitude of excessive dedication to work
    
- Effort-reward imbalance: ratio between effort and reward
    

## How it is applied

Self-administered questionnaire with Likert scales (usually 4 or 5 points). The full version has about 22 items (17 for effort/reward plus 5 for overcommitment). Short versions exist.

## How it was used in the studies

- [[Monstadt et al. (2026)]]: Used for psychosocial stress assessment (effort-reward imbalance) in a German population-based study.
    
- [[Niedhammer et al. (2024)]]: Reward scale of the ERI Model to measure esteem, promotion, and job security.
    

## Advantages and limitations

**Advantages:** Solid theoretical basis, validated internationally, captures dimensions of recognition and organizational justice, relevant to burnout.

**Limitations:** Does not capture all types of reward (e.g., flexibility, autonomy); requires adaptation to remote work contexts, where rewards may be less visible.
