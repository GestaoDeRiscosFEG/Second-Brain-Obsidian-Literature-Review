### Klein and Zwilling (2024)

#### Reference (ABNT)
KLEIN, Galit; ZWILLING, Moti. The weakest link: employee cyber-defense behaviors while working from home. **Journal of Computer Information Systems**, v. 64, n. 3, p. 408-422, 2024. DOI: 10.1080/08874417.2023.2221200.

#### Objective
To investigate the cognitive and organizational antecedents of employee active cyber defense behaviors and cyber risk behaviors, examining how corporate cybersecurity training programs and the physical work location (working from home vs. on-site at enterprise premises) moderate risk-taking in digital environments.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Does cyberthreat awareness relate to employee cyber defense behaviors via the mediation of cybersecurity knowledge? Does organizational cybersecurity training serially enhance defense behaviors through threat awareness and technical knowledge? How does the work location (home vs. company premises) moderate the relationship between cybersecurity knowledge, defense tool implementation, and cyber risk behaviors?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Cross-sectional quantitative survey analyzed with conditional process modeling (SPSS PROCESS macro)
*   **Application:** Structured online survey administered via i-panel to 338 full-time Israeli employees (261 teleworking from home at least part-time, 77 on-site) across regulated and non-regulated corporate sectors during and following the COVID-19 pandemic
*   **Type of work:** Work from home (telework), on-site corporate work, and hybrid arrangements

#### Main findings
Organizational cybersecurity training significantly improves employee cyber defense behaviors through a serial mediation mechanism: training increases cyberthreat awareness (beta = 0.17, p < 0.04), which fosters cybersecurity knowledge (beta = 0.46, p < 0.01), ultimately leading to higher active deployment of defense tools (beta = 0.24, p < 0.05). Physical work location and installed defense mechanisms exerted a complex moderating effect on cyber risk behaviors: teleworkers with limited defense tools and low knowledge exhibited high cyber risk behaviors due to unmonitored home networks. Unexpectedly, on-site employees working in defense-heavy organizations also exhibited high cyber risk behaviors; the authors explain this via an overconfidence effect, wherein employees transfer security responsibility to corporate firewalls and IT departments, resulting in careless digital behavior.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Organizational, Cognitive, Psychosocial
*   **Specific risk factors:** Unmonitored domestic IT networks, family/friend access to corporate laptops, password reuse, disabling antivirus software to download files, phishing susceptibility, cognitive overconfidence in institutional protections, and lack of supervisory oversight
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Enterprise data breaches, ransomware infections, corporate espionage, intellectual property theft, severe financial losses, and legal liability under data privacy regulations

#### Risk management strategies
Designing and executing comprehensive, continuous employee cybersecurity training programs adapted to remote work realities. Moving beyond passive instruction toward active, simulated phishing campaigns (e.g., IronScales). Mandating multi-factor authentication (MFA) and hard disk encryption on all devices accessing enterprise assets. Establishing clear, enforceable home office cyber policies governing personal device usage and home router Wi-Fi security. Fostering a security culture that overcomes employee overconfidence and emphasizes shared responsibility.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Cyberthreat Awareness Construct]]: 16-item scale measuring awareness of diverse digital hazards (phishing, DDoS, SQL injection, malware).
- [[Cybersecurity Knowledge Scale]]: 16-item self-efficacy instrument evaluating practical knowledge required to protect organizational digital assets.
- [[Cyber Risk Behavior Index]]: 20-item behavioral checklist adapted from Hadlington (2018) measuring frequency of risky online practices.
- [[Cyber Defense Behaviors Scale]]: 5-item index measuring active deployment of protective mechanisms (two-factor authentication, antivirus, automatic screen lock).

#### Relation to regulations
The study analyzes compliance with Israeli National Cyber Directorate regulatory standards, categorizing organizations based on statutory oversight criteria (enterprise size, critical infrastructure sectors, service breadth). It also discusses alignment with international security standards such as ISO/IEC 27001 and NIST SP 800-53.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The study treated diverse cyber threats and defense tools as aggregated unidimensional constructs, pointing to the need for future studies to defragment specific risk behaviors (e.g., social engineering vs. software vulnerabilities). The research did not objectively measure the managerial enforcement intensity of cyber policies within firms. In addition, the sample was restricted to Israeli workers, an environment with distinct national cyber defense investments; cross-cultural comparative research is needed to evaluate how cultural dimensions (such as uncertainty avoidance) influence remote worker cyber hygiene.

#### Other relevant information
The authors ground their conceptual model in the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) and Protection Motivation Theory (PMT).
