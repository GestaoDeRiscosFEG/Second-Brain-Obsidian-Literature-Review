### Cheung (2024)

#### Reference (ABNT)
CHEUNG, Victor K. L. Practical considerations of workplace wellbeing management under post-pandemic work-from-home conditions. **International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health**, v. 21, n. 7, p. 924, 2024. DOI: 10.3390/ijerph21070924.

#### Objective
This essay aims to address major considerations and challenges regarding the transformation and redesign of work arrangements in the post-pandemic context, providing evidence-based recommendations to inform practical managerial decisions for optimizing overall employee wellbeing under work-from-home conditions.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Do work-from-home conditions lead to positive behavioral changes in health outcomes? Do personality types matter in adaptation to work-from-home conditions? Which form of conflict has a greater impact on work-family outcomes, family interference with work or work interference with family? Application of the classical scarcity hypothesis to resource allocation between professional and domestic spheres.

#### Method
*   **Type:** Literature review and conceptual essay
*   **Application:** Synthesis of empirical literature and official workforce statistics from the United Kingdom, the United States, and Hong Kong, including reference samples such as 843 university staff members evaluating home ergonomics, 27 full-time Swedish workers examining sleep patterns, and over 5,000 corporate employees in a McKinsey remote work survey
*   **Type of work:** Work from home (telework)

#### Main findings
Female teleworkers report higher life satisfaction and better family relationships when domestic chores and family responsibilities are shared equitably with partners. A positive association exists between the number of children and family interference with work. Conscientious employees may experience greater vulnerability to burnout and turnover in unstructured remote settings due to role ambiguity and difficulty disconnecting, whereas employees with lower conscientiousness and higher openness adapt more readily. High communication transparency regarding organizational visions and telework policies corresponds to a two- to fivefold increase in perceived wellbeing and productivity. Chronic stress and non-standardized home workstations correlate with musculoskeletal disorders, sleep disruption, and loneliness.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial
*   **Specific risk factors:** Chronic musculoskeletal discomfort, severe low back pain, head and neck discomfort, occupational stress, anxiety, burnout, loneliness, blur of work-life boundaries, work-family conflict, cyberbullying, and virtual workplace violence
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Excessive alcohol consumption, cardiovascular complications, eating disorders, weight gain, sleep disturbance, impaired psychological wellbeing, reduced organizational productivity, and increased healthcare costs

#### Risk management strategies
Implementation of the British Psychological Society SHARE framework (Safe homeworking, Help yourself and others, Adapt to change, Relieve the pressure, Evaluate). Performance of preliminary home workstation eligibility assessments covering display screen equipment, seating, and cybersecurity. Provision of supplementary ergonomic equipment by employers or arrangement of standardized rental/co-working spaces when home environments are suboptimal. Establishment of explicit boundary and switch-off policies. Offering psychoeducation workshops, stress management coaching, peer-support channels, and secondary/tertiary behavioral health interventions.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[SHARE Model]]: Applied as a psychological framework comprising five operational pillars (Safe homeworking, Help yourself and others, Adapt to change, Relieve the pressure, Evaluate) to guide managerial decision-making and sustainable work-from-home arrangements.

#### Relation to regulations
not reported

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Whether work-from-home is a suitable long-term working mode for densely populated metropolitan areas remains inconclusive. Further research during the post-pandemic period should develop models examining the degree to which sleep duration explains wellbeing outcomes and interacts with physical activity and quality of life. The potential downsides of remote working regarding creative collaboration and team diversity require deeper investigation. Due to substantial sample variation across synthesized literature, the interrelatedness of contributing factors and their collective explanatory power over overall teleworker wellbeing remain to be established.

#### Other relevant information
The study details the SHARE model developed in conjunction with British Psychological Society guidance for remote work well-being.
