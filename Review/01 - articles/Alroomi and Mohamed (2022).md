### Alroomi and Mohamed (2022)

#### Reference (ABNT)
ALROOMI, Anwar S.; MOHAMED, Sherif. Does fatigue mediate the relation between physical isolation and safety behaviour among isolated oil and gas workers? **Safety Science**, v. 147, art. 105639, p. 1-10, 2022. DOI: 10.1016/j.ssci.2021.105639.

#### Objective
To investigate whether physical and mental fatigue mediate the relationship between physical isolation variables (objective social isolation and subjective loneliness) and occupational safety behavior (safety compliance and safety participation) among workers stationed in remote oil and gas field sites.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Does physical fatigue mediate the relationship between social isolation/loneliness and safety compliance or participation? Does mental fatigue mediate the relationship between social isolation/loneliness and safety compliance or participation? How does geographical and psychosocial isolation impair workers' adherence to occupational safety protocols?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Cross-sectional quantitative survey
*   **Application:** Paper-and-pencil survey administered to 387 foreign expatriate workers (76.2% Indian) living in residential work camps adjacent to isolated oil and gas production fields in Kuwait
*   **Type of work:** Remote, isolated site-based work

#### Main findings
Hierarchical regression analyses revealed that loneliness significantly predicted both physical fatigue (r = 0.480, p < 0.001) and mental fatigue (r = 0.560, p < 0.001). However, only mental fatigue functioned as a statistically significant mediator between loneliness and safety behaviors, partially mediating both safety compliance (b = -0.146, p = 0.001) and safety participation (b = -0.141, p < 0.01). In contrast, objective social isolation did not predict fatigue, and physical fatigue did not significantly predict safety compliance or participation. Workers experiencing acute loneliness and mental fatigue showed impaired cognitive vigilance, leading to higher rates of safety violations and elevated accident risks.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Psychosocial, Physical, Organizational, Cognitive
*   **Specific risk factors:** Geographical remoteness, separation from family, subjective loneliness, cognitive exhaustion, mental fatigue, reduced situational awareness, and unsafe shortcuts
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Impaired cognitive control, increased workplace accident frequency, physical trauma, severe psychological distress, and occupational injury

#### Risk management strategies
Implementing continuous fatigue monitoring protocols to assess cognitive and emotional strain. Providing on-site professional psychological counseling services to address loneliness and psychological exhaustion. Organizing structured bi-weekly or monthly social excursions outside the remote camp into urban centers to restore social connectedness. Enhancing internet infrastructure to facilitate regular communication with family members.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[LSNS-R]]: Abbreviated Lubben Social Network Scale measuring objective social network size and contact frequency.
- [[UCLA Loneliness Scale]]: Short version measuring subjective workplace feelings of isolation.
- [[FAS]] / [[Fatigue Questionnaire (FQ)]] / [[Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory (MFI)]]: 14-item composite measure assessing physical and mental fatigue dimensions.

#### Relation to regulations
The study was conducted under Full Research Ethics Clearance (2018/852) from Griffith University and followed international occupational safety research guidelines.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The cross-sectional design prevents establishing definitive causal trajectories between isolation, fatigue, and safety incidents. Reliance on self-reported measures may introduce common method variance and subjective reporting bias. The sample was restricted to expatriate male workers in Kuwait, requiring caution when generalizing to other industrial settings. Future research should separate the construct of energy from fatigue and examine sleep architecture longitudinally.

#### Other relevant information
The authors explain that increasing industrial automation relieves workers from physical exhaustion while simultaneously elevating cognitive vigilance demands, making mental fatigue far more hazardous than physical tiredness in modern remote operations.
