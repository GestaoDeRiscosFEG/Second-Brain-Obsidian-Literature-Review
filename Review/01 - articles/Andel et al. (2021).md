### Andel et al. (2021)

#### Reference (ABNT)
ANDEL, Shawn A.; SHEN, Winny; ARVAN, Michelle L. Depending on your own kindness: the moderating role of self-compassion on the within-person consequences of work loneliness during the COVID-19 pandemic. **Journal of Occupational Health Psychology**, v. 26, n. 4, p. 276-290, 2021. DOI: 10.1037/ocp0000271.

#### Objective
To examine how pandemic-related work stressors (telecommuting frequency, job insecurity, and lack of informational justice) relate to weekly fluctuations in work loneliness, evaluate the downstream effects of loneliness on depressive symptoms and organizational citizenship behaviors (OCB), and test whether self-compassion buffers these relationships.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Is weekly telecommuting frequency positively associated with weekly work loneliness? Does work loneliness mediate relationships between work stressors and employee depression or OCB? Does employee self-compassion attenuate the detrimental effects of work loneliness on psychological well-being and prosocial behaviors?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Longitudinal repeated-measures survey design across 8 consecutive weeks
*   **Application:** Weekly tracking of 265 full-time employees recruited via Prolific across 44 U.S. states between March and May 2020, analyzed using multilevel path analysis and moderated mediation modeling
*   **Type of work:** Remote telework and hybrid arrangements

#### Main findings
Multilevel path analysis demonstrated that weekly telecommuting frequency (gamma = 0.43, p < 0.01), job insecurity (gamma = 0.09, p < 0.01), and a lack of COVID-related informational justice (gamma = 0.07, p < 0.05) were each independently associated with increased weekly work loneliness. In turn, heightened weekly work loneliness significantly predicted elevated depressive symptoms (gamma = 0.09, p < 0.01) and decreased engagement in organizational citizenship behaviors (gamma = -0.10, p < 0.05). Trait self-compassion buffered the relationship between loneliness and depression, with highly self-compassionate employees exhibiting lower depressive reactions to isolation. However, unexpectedly, highly self-compassionate workers reduced their OCBs when feeling lonely, reflecting adaptive conservation of depleted emotional resources.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Psychosocial, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** High telecommuting frequency, physical and social isolation, lack of informal face-to-face interactions, ambiguous computer-mediated communication, job insecurity, and perceived organizational opacity
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Clinical depressive symptoms, psychological distress, withdrawal of prosocial helping behaviors, citizenship fatigue, and reduced organizational performance

#### Risk management strategies
Establishing transparent and timely corporate communications regarding strategic decisions to enhance informational justice. Structuring intentional opportunities for informal virtual socialization (virtual coffee breaks, online team lunches). Protecting employee tenure and clarifying job expectations to reduce perceived job insecurity. Facilitating organizational training programs in self-compassion and mindfulness to help remote workers cultivate adaptive psychological coping mechanisms.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[UCLA Loneliness Scale]]: Adapted 5-item domain-specific instrument measuring weekly feelings of workplace social isolation and lack of companionship.
- [[DASS-21]]: Depression subscale (7 items) from the Depression Anxiety Stress Scales assessing weekly depressive symptomatology.
- [[Self-Compassion Scale (SCS-SF)]]: 12-item short form assessing self-kindness, common humanity, and mindfulness.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Extant loneliness literature predominantly focuses on chronic, general trait loneliness or interpersonal peer dynamics, neglecting transient weekly fluctuations in work-domain loneliness and connection to the organization as an entity. The authors highlight limitations regarding the extraordinary pandemic time frame and reliance on self-report metrics. Future research should examine alternative mediators such as feelings of powerlessness and test whether psychological buffers hold during post-crisis telework.

#### Other relevant information
The authors emphasize that even transient, weekly states of work loneliness act as an established sentiment capable of triggering maladaptive regulatory withdrawal cycles rather than prosocial reconnection.
