### Benavides et al. (2021)

#### Reference (ABNT)
BENAVIDES, Fernando G., et al. The future of work after the COVID-19, the uncertain role of teleworking at home. **Revista Brasileira de Saúde Ocupacional**, v. 46, art. e27, p. 1-8, 2021. DOI: 10.1590/2317-6369000037820.

#### Objective
To reflect upon the conceptual meaning, magnitude, and evolving trends of teleworking from home before and during the COVID-19 pandemic, analyzing its potential impacts on worker health, safety, and well-being, and identifying regulatory and epidemiological monitoring frameworks for post-pandemic labor governance.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
How has the transition to home telework transformed employment relationships and occupational risk exposures? What are the primary legal, practical, and constitutional obstacles to enforcing occupational safety standards within private households? How can national surveys on working conditions and employment systematically incorporate telework indicators to generate scientific evidence for labor legislation?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Narrative essay, critical literature review, and institutional analysis
*   **Application:** Comparative review of telework prevalence data, statutory frameworks, and occupational health trends across Latin America, the Caribbean (Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Panama), and the European Union
*   **Type of work:** Home-based telework and remote work

#### Main findings
While home telework provided vital emergency continuity during lockdowns, it introduced serious occupational health challenges that remain poorly regulated. The authors distinguish telework from broader remote work, noting that home-based telework blurs the boundaries between domestic life and employment. The expansion of telework was accompanied by extended working hours, difficulty disconnecting, heightened social isolation, ergonomic deficiencies leading to musculoskeletal disorders, and elevated stress. In Latin America, structural inequalities—such as high informal employment, deficient internet connectivity, and weak social protection—amplify vulnerabilities, disproportionately affecting female workers who shoulder unequal burdens of unpaid caregiving. Furthermore, constitutional protections regarding the inviolability of the home hinder physical labor inspections, creating an enforcement vacuum for employer safety obligations.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** Display screen equipment misuse, prolonged sitting, non-neutral postures, blurred spatial and temporal boundaries, work-family conflict, social and professional isolation, inability to disconnect, extended working hours, and domestic injury hazards (slips, falls, electrical issues)
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Work-related musculoskeletal disorders, chronic occupational stress, anxiety, depressive symptoms, sleep disturbances, unhealthy lifestyle habits (increased tobacco/alcohol use), and digital presenteeism

#### Risk management strategies
Enacting comprehensive statutory legislation on telework that guarantees equal labor rights, mandatory employer provision and maintenance of ergonomic equipment, and strict working time limits. Incorporating specific, standardized telework questions into national Surveys on Working Conditions, Employment, and Health (CTESLAC). Establishing negotiated labor inspection protocols that balance workplace safety verification with constitutional rights to domestic privacy. Promoting collective bargaining agreements that enforce the right to digital disconnection and establish transparent employer cost compensation.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[CTESLAC Working Conditions Questionnaire]]: Standardized survey model for Latin America and the Caribbean incorporating ILO-recommended items to capture home telework characteristics, workstation locations, and health outcomes.

#### Relation to regulations
The study examines historical and contemporary regulatory benchmarks, including ILO Convention 177 on Home Work (1996), the 2002 European Framework Agreement on Telework, the eLAC2020 Digital Agenda, and national telework statutes enacted in Argentina (Law 27.555), Spain (Royal Decree-Law 28/2020), Brazil (Law 13.467/2017), Costa Rica, Chile, and Panama.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The absence of systematic literature reviews on telework health effects severely limits evidence-based policymaking across diverse regulatory and socio-economic contexts. There is a lack of empirical epidemiological data on how telework interacts with informal and precarious employment in developing regions. Furthermore, the conflict between employer safety responsibilities and the constitutional inviolability of private homes remains unresolved in labor doctrine. The authors stress the urgent need for standardized questions in national working conditions surveys to monitor long-term health trajectories.

#### Other relevant information
The essay is a collaborative contribution authored by the SALTRA network and academic institutions across Spain, Argentina, Chile, Honduras, Costa Rica, Panama, and the United States.
