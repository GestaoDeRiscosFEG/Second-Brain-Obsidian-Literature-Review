### Matisāne et al. (2021)

#### Reference (ABNT)
MATISĀNE, Linda, et al. Challenges for workplace risk assessment in home offices - results from a qualitative descriptive study on working life during the first wave of the COVID-19 pandemic in Latvia. **International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health**, v. 18, n. 20, art. 10876, p. 1-19, 2021. DOI: 10.3390/ijerph182010876.

#### Objective
To analyze the practical experiences, barriers, and strategies of employers and occupational safety and health (OSH) experts in conducting workplace risk assessments in home offices in Latvia during the first wave of the COVID-19 pandemic, and to identify alternative methodologies to support healthy remote working.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
What measures were implemented by employers at the organizational level to ensure safe and healthy teleworking conditions? How did organizations conduct workplace risk assessments for telework, and what tools or experts were involved? What legal, operational, and privacy barriers hindered employers from fulfilling their statutory safety obligations in employees' homes?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Qualitative descriptive focus group study
*   **Application:** Ten structured focus group discussions with 89 participants in Latvia (65 employers/managers and 24 OSH experts representing both small-to-medium enterprises and large corporations across diverse regional and economic sectors)
*   **Type of work:** Telework and remote work from home

#### Main findings
A severe deficit in statutory compliance was documented: 71.9% of focus group participants acknowledged that working conditions of teleworkers were not inspected or evaluated by employers during the first wave of the pandemic. Primary barriers included confusion regarding ambiguous telework legislation, bureaucratic over-regulation, lack of time during business survival crises, and domestic privacy concerns (GDPR and employee reluctance to share images of private living quarters). Even certified OSH specialists reported confusion regarding how to legally conduct remote assessments. When assessments were performed, successful organizations abandoned physical site visits in favor of alternative digital methods: self-administered checklists, employee-submitted photos or short videos, and telephone consultations. Crucially, survey data showed that employees whose employers conducted risk assessments reported significantly fewer health complaints, whereas workers without assessments exhibited significantly higher odds of anxiety (OR = 2.83), eye strain (OR = 2.81), and persistent musculoskeletal pain (OR = 1.82).

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial, Organizational, Physical
*   **Specific risk factors:** Suboptimal and improvised workstations (working on sofas, kitchen counters, or ironing boards), non-adjustable chairs, lack of armrests, improper display screen heights, poor lighting and ventilation, legal uncertainty, domestic privacy conflicts, and lack of professional OSH oversight
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Work-related musculoskeletal disorders, chronic back and neck pain lasting over three days, eye strain, elevated psychological anxiety, and long-term deterioration of physical and mental health

#### Risk management strategies
Deployment of self-assessment checklists distributed to employees to evaluate physical workstations, illumination, and electrical safety. Execution of remote ergonomic evaluations using standardized photos (one from the side and one from directly behind the user) or short video recordings evaluated by OSH experts. Delivery of tailored online training modules and concise educational infographics focusing on home workstation adjustments and micro-breaks. Permitting employees to transport office equipment (ergonomic chairs, height-adjustable desks, monitors, and input devices) from corporate premises to home offices. Providing structured individual feedback with viable, low-cost corrective recommendations following risk reviews.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Home Office Self-Assessment Checklist]]: A structured diagnostic questionnaire adapted from guidelines developed by the Latvian Ministry of Welfare and the State Labour Inspectorate, allowing employees to self-evaluate home workstations, indoor climate, space dimensions, and lighting.

#### Relation to regulations
The study analyzes national compliance with the Latvian Labour Protection Law and European Council Directive 89/391/EEC (Framework Directive), emphasizing that statutory health and safety obligations remain identical regardless of whether work is performed on employer premises or in private homes. It notes that the Framework Directive mandates that safety measures must not involve financial costs for workers.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The feasibility, legal validity, and long-term effectiveness of remote and virtual assessment methods (photos, videos, and self-checklists) in the post-pandemic era require formal validation to establish standardized OSH operational protocols. OSH professional education programs must be updated to prepare specialists for non-standard, distributed work environments. Furthermore, research should investigate how to prevent a growing disparity where high-performing organizations with established OSH systems improve while resource-constrained small and medium-sized enterprises fail to implement basic telework protections.

#### Other relevant information
The authors note that the transition to telework necessitates shifting from general collective risk assessments toward highly personalized individual assessments, given that domestic environments exhibit substantial inter-individual variation.
