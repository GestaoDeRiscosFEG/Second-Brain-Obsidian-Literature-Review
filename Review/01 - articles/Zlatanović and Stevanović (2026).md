### Zlatanović and Stevanović (2026)

#### Reference (ABNT)
ZLATANOVIĆ, Sanja; STEVANOVIĆ, Anđelija. Resilience in labor regulation: evaluating Serbia's post-pandemic occupational safety and health reform. **Review of European and Comparative Law**, v. 64, n. 1, p. 153-168, 2026. DOI: 10.31743/recl.19516.

#### Objective
To evaluate Serbia's post-pandemic occupational safety and health (OSH) legislative reforms using legal-theoretical, normative, and comparative doctrinal methods, integrating resilience management theory into legal analysis to assess alignment with European standards and critically evaluate the regulatory framework's capacity to anticipate, absorb, adapt to, and recover from systemic disruptions such as pandemics and accelerated workplace digitalization.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Does the Serbian OSH Act of 2023 effectively guarantee worker safety and health across evolving physical, remote, and hybrid work modalities, given its persistent neglect of psychosocial risks and mental health? How resilient is national labor regulation in absorbing and adapting to systemic shocks without eroding employee protections?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Qualitative legal analysis, theoretical-normative inquiry, and comparative doctrinal legal review
*   **Application:** Comprehensive doctrinal and normative analysis of Serbian labor statutes, European Union OSH directives, and comparative post-pandemic legal reforms applicable to all economic sectors across on-site, hybrid, and remote employment arrangements
*   **Type of work:** Hybrid, remote, and on-site work

#### Main findings
Applying the lens of resilience management theory, the analysis reveals that Serbia's post-pandemic OSH reforms—centered on the OSH Act of 2023—failed to sufficiently enhance the adaptive, absorptive, or transformative capacities of the national labor protection system. Although temporary ad-hoc governmental decrees addressed immediate emergency needs during the acute phase of COVID-19, the post-pandemic reform failed to codify lasting protections for flexible, hybrid, and digital work. Crucially, the 2023 legislation omitted explicit statutory definitions, risk assessment mandates, and preventive employer duties concerning psychosocial hazards and mental health (such as chronic stress, anxiety, depression, and burnout), perpetuating a traditional physical-hazard bias. Consequently, the national regulatory system remains largely reactive and vulnerable rather than resilient, leaving hybrid and remote workers in a regulatory gray zone with diminished protections.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Psychosocial, Ergonomic, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** High occupational stress, chronic anxiety, clinical depression, emotional burnout, cognitive fatigue, ergonomic discomfort from uninspected home setups, regulatory omission of psychosocial risk assessments, and lack of emergency labor mechanisms
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Deterioration of employee mental health, impaired job performance, decreased quality of life, institutional instability during public health crises, and loss of acquired labor protections

#### Risk management strategies
Reforming national OSH legislation to integrate resilience management principles into statutory labor codes. Codifying explicit, mandatory employer obligations to identify, assess, and mitigate psychosocial risks and mental health hazards across physical and remote workspaces. Establishing formal statutory mechanisms and pre-defined administrative protocols for emergency labor regulation during crises. Aligning national labor statutes with European Union Framework Directive 89/391/EEC and international mental health standards. Shifting corporate and regulatory compliance from passive reactive responses toward proactive, adaptive risk prevention.

#### Relation to regulations
The study focuses on the Serbian Occupational Safety and Health Act of 2023 (Zakon o bezbednosti i zdravlju na radu), evaluating its degree of harmonization with European Union Council Directive 89/391/EEC (Framework Directive) and European social acquis. It analyzes the constitutional and administrative mechanisms governing emergency decrees and evaluates international comparative legal benchmarks regarding statutory rights to mental health protection at work.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The primary statutory gap identified is the complete legislative omission of non-physical and psychosocial risks within the updated OSH statute, maintaining a narrow 20th-century focus on mechanical, chemical, and physical workplace accidents. The authors criticize the discontinuity of labor protections, as temporary pandemic provisions were discontinued without enacting permanent statutory protections for remote and hybrid workers. The study notes that legal doctrine must develop integrated interdisciplinary frameworks combining legal theory with organizational resilience science to evaluate future labor reforms in candidate EU member states.

#### Other relevant information
The study is grounded in legal science and labor law doctrine, emphasizing that statutory resilience requires regulatory systems that evolve with changing employment paradigms rather than retrofitting obsolete physical rules onto digital and hybrid work.
