### Nagata et al. (2021)

#### Reference (ABNT)
NAGATA, Tomohisa, et al. Anticipated health effects and proposed countermeasures following the immediate introduction of telework in response to the spread of COVID-19: the findings of a rapid health impact assessment in Japan. **Journal of Occupational Health**, v. 63, n. 1, art. e12198, p. 1-15, 2021. DOI: 10.1002/1348-9585.12198.

#### Objective
To develop and implement a rapid five-step Health Impact Assessment (HIA) in Japan immediately following the emergency declaration for the COVID-19 pandemic, evaluating positive and negative health effects of sudden telework adoption across teleworkers, non-teleworkers, and family members, and formulating practical, easily implementable organizational countermeasures.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
What are the anticipated positive and negative health impacts of sudden, widespread telework introduction across three distinct stakeholder groups? How can expert occupational health physicians prioritize emerging physical and psychosocial risks in the absence of mature empirical datasets? What immediate countermeasures can corporate leadership execute to mitigate hazards while preserving the operational benefits of remote work?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Rapid mixed-methods Health Impact Assessment (HIA) following a structured five-stage procedure: screening, scoping, appraisal, reporting, and monitoring/evaluation
*   **Application:** Nationwide multi-sector occupational evaluation in Japan conducted between March 31 and May 1, 2020. The appraisal phase incorporated structured interviews with five certified Senior Occupational Health Physicians reflecting feedback from corporate managers and employees, with subsequent validation by an independent panel of six occupational health professionals
*   **Type of work:** Telework, on-site essential work, and domestic environments (comparative triadic framework)

#### Main findings
The rapid HIA successfully mapped 115 distinct health effects across four core life domains (work, lifestyle, disease and medical care, home and community), comprising 59 items for teleworkers, 29 for non-teleworkers, and 27 for family members. For teleworkers, critical high-priority negative impacts included eye, shoulder, and back strain from prolonged sitting; stress from rapid onboarding of unvetted software; social isolation from reduced communication; failure of supervisors to detect early depressive deterioration; and presenteeism. For non-teleworkers, high-priority issues centered on infection anxieties and feelings of unfairness and frustration over being ineligible for telework. For family members, stress from living in cramped quarters and increased domestic chores predominated. Conversely, significant positive effects were identified, including elimination of commuting fatigue, reduced traffic accident risks, and increased family interaction.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Physical, Psychosocial, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** Visual fatigue, neck and shoulder stiffness, lower back pain from dining furniture, physical inactivity, disrupted circadian sleep rhythms, unhealthy diet, increased alcohol consumption and domestic smoking, feelings of isolation, fear of IT security breaches, and resentment among on-site staff
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Work-related musculoskeletal disorders, chronic psychological distress, exacerbation of depressive symptoms, sleep disorders, presenteeism, productivity loss, and interpersonal domestic friction

#### Risk management strategies
Distribution of self-educational ergonomic checklists guiding workstation setup and break scheduling in accordance with national VDT guidelines. Introduction of structured daily life record sheets and standardized morning and evening virtual check-in meetings to anchor daily work-rest rhythms. Promotion of home exercise routines (incorporating municipal physical activity programs and radio calisthenics). Establishing objective task management tools rather than subjective digital surveillance. Establishing clear safety guidelines, workload prioritization, and clinical consultation channels for on-site non-teleworkers and medically vulnerable staff. Encouraging equitable redistribution of domestic responsibilities among household members.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Rapid Health Impact Assessment (HIA)]]: A 5-stage prospective decision-support framework (screening, scoping, appraisal, reporting, evaluation) adapted to rapid crisis timelines to systematically catalog, prioritize, and mitigate emerging workplace health impacts.

#### Relation to regulations
The study aligns with the Japanese Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) Guidelines for Occupational Health Management in Information Equipment Work (2019) regarding video display terminal (VDT) ergonomics. It also references governmental pandemic subsidies established to assist enterprises in subsidizing telework infrastructure and teleconsultation services.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The rapid crisis implementation of the HIA relied on expert occupational medical consensus rather than exhaustive quantitative longitudinal measurements of clinical health outcomes. Monitoring and evaluation of telework interventions must be continuous, involving iterative revisions as work arrangements evolve. In addition, the authors emphasize a severe equity gap: workplaces and small-to-medium enterprises that lack qualified occupational health physicians and nurses cannot effectively implement these multi-component preventive countermeasures, leaving their teleworkers disproportionately exposed to emerging hazards over extended time horizons.

#### Other relevant information
The assessment is notable for pioneering a triadic evaluation model that considers not only remote workers, but also the health impacts on on-site employees and domestic cohabitants.
