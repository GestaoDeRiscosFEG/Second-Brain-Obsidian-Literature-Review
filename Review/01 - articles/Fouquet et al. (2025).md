### Fouquet et al. (2025)

#### Reference (ABNT)
FOUQUET, Natacha, et al. Onset of low back pain and teleworking conditions during lockdown in France: a structural equation modelling approach. **Annals of Work Exposures and Health**, v. 69, n. 5, p. 531-543, 2025. DOI: 10.1093/annweh/wxaf007.

#### Objective
To examine the complex relationships between teleworking conditions (material setup and telework intensity), depressive symptomatology, and the new onset of low back pain (LBP) during France's third national COVID-19 lockdown in spring 2021, using a structural equation modelling (SEM) approach.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Do material working conditions at home directly influence the onset of low back pain? Does satisfaction with teleworking conditions mediate the relationship between teleworking arrangements, depressive symptoms, and musculoskeletal pain? How do individual factors (age, gender, obesity) interact with mental health and spinal discomfort?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Cross-sectional survey evaluated with Structural Equation Modelling (SEM)
*   **Application:** Subsample of 466 teleworkers drawn from the representative national CoviPrev cohort (n = 2,093 working adults) who worked remotely at least one day per week during France's third lockdown and were completely free of low back pain at baseline
*   **Type of work:** Remote telework and hybrid work

#### Main findings
The structural equation model demonstrated two distinct pathways linking teleworking conditions to the onset of low back pain: a direct biomechanical pathway (standardized coefficient = 0.24, p < 0.05) and an indirect pathway mediated by satisfaction with teleworking conditions and depressive symptoms (mediated effect size = -0.08, p < 0.001). Higher satisfaction with telework was significantly associated with a reduced risk of depressive symptoms (-0.43, p < 0.0001), which in turn strongly predicted low back pain (0.58, p < 0.0001). Surprisingly, having a second external monitor was associated with higher LBP incidence (10.6% vs 3.4%, p = 0.002), likely reflecting extended screen exposure, higher task prescription, and increased sedentary sitting. Biological sex was a strong independent predictor: female teleworkers exhibited more than double the incidence of new-onset LBP compared to males (9.5% vs 3.9%, p = 0.017), attributable to unequal domestic burdens and less ergonomic home setups.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** Unsuitable home workstations, prolonged uninterrupted sitting, lack of posture variation, high screen time, second monitor misuse, depressive symptoms, psychological distress, and social isolation
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Work-related musculoskeletal disorders, new-onset chronic low back pain, physical fatigue, emotional exhaustion, and increased sickness absenteeism

#### Risk management strategies
Employer provision of standardized, adjustable ergonomic hardware for home offices (monitors, chairs, and external keyboards) guided by official prevention standards (INRS). Implementing organizational adjustments that encourage and mandate regular movement breaks to combat prolonged static sitting. Integrating mental health screening and psychological support services into occupational risk prevention. Adopting multidimensional prevention frameworks that combine physical ergonomics, job satisfaction enhancements, and gender-sensitive workload management.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[HADS]]: Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (7 depression items) used to model the latent construct of depressive symptomatology.
- [[Nordic Musculoskeletal Questionnaire (NMQ)]]: Adapted standardized binary screening item investigating new-onset low back pain lasting at least several hours.
- [[Material Teleworking Conditions Checklist]]: 4-item binary indicator evaluating dedicated work room, second display screen, office chair, and external keyboard.

#### Relation to regulations
The study discusses French national lockdown emergency decrees and aligns with the French National Research and Safety Institute (INRS 2020) occupational prevention guidelines for teleworking.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The cross-sectional design prevents definitive assertions regarding causality, as reverse causality between depressive mood and pain perception cannot be ruled out. The study could not analyze broader organizational constraints, such as job autonomy, performance metrics, or whether teleworking was voluntary or imposed. Furthermore, the survey did not record technical screen placement parameters (height, tilt, viewing distance) or the ergonomic adjustability of home chairs. The authors emphasize the need for longitudinal prospective studies tracking hybrid workers over extended post-pandemic horizons.

#### Other relevant information
The study is the first to empirically demonstrate through SEM that psychological depression acts as a major mediating bridge through which poor remote working conditions translate into physical spinal disorders.
