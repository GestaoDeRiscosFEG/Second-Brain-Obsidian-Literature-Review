### Corneliuson et al. (2026)

#### Reference (ABNT)
CORNELIUSON, Caroline, et al. Psychosocial work experience after implementing hybrid work: a longitudinal study. **BMC Public Health**, v. 26, art. 1359, p. 1-9, 2026. DOI: 10.1186/s12889-026-27487-x.

#### Objective
To investigate the longitudinal impact of the transition to hybrid work on the psychosocial work experience among white-collar employees, and to determine how personal and occupational factors (such as age, sex, managerial position, presence of children at home, task characteristics, autonomy, and time spent working at the office) influenced changes in work experience over time.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
What is the longitudinal impact of transitioning to hybrid work on employees' psychosocial work experience? In what manner do personal and occupational factors influence changes in this work experience over time?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Longitudinal quantitative survey
*   **Application:** White-collar workers in a medium-sized Swedish municipality (56,000 inhabitants) tracked across two time points: baseline in 2017 (pre-pandemic activity-based office setting) and follow-up in late 2023 (1.5 years after lifting national pandemic restrictions; n = 148, with 54.4% having children living at home)
*   **Type of work:** Hybrid work (alternating between office and home)

#### Main findings
The transition to hybrid work resulted in a statistically significant improvement in the overall Work Experience Measurement Scale (WEMS) score over time (p = 0.008), with notable gains across four specific dimensions: supportive working conditions (p = 0.021), autonomy (p = 0.005), time experience (p < 0.001), and leadership (p = 0.039). The time experience dimension, reflecting the ability to finish work without excessive time pressure and avoid working beyond scheduled hours, showed the most consistent improvement across all items. A central finding was a significant sex disparity: women experienced a marked, statistically significant improvement in overall work experience over time (+4.3 points, 95% CI: 1.7 to 6.8), whereas men's scores did not change significantly. Managerial position, degree of autonomy, and the number of days worked remotely were not significant explanatory factors for overall score changes.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial, Cognitive, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** High mental workload, burnout vulnerability, noise distractions in open-plan offices, blurred boundaries between work and personal life, and work-family conflict
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Role stress, impaired recovery from work, potential overtime accumulation, and work-to-family interference

#### Risk management strategies
Implementation of a formal post-pandemic hybrid work framework offering flexible remote work options (typically 2 to 3 days weekly at the office). Employer provision of ergonomic hardware (computer monitors, input devices, and adjustable office chairs) for home workstations upon employee request. Promotion of regular supervisor check-ins and accessible managerial leadership. Combining activity-based flexible office design with the autonomy to select working locations suited to specific concentration demands.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[WEMS]]: The Swedish Work Experience Measurement Scale (32 items across 6 dimensions: supportive working conditions, internal work experience, autonomy, time experience, leadership, and process of change), administered at baseline and follow-up to measure psychosocial work experience from a salutogenic perspective.

#### Relation to regulations
The study discusses organizational compliance with national COVID-19 work-from-home restrictions issued by the Public Health Agency of Sweden, which were formally lifted in spring 2022.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Extant hybrid work literature relies predominantly on cross-sectional datasets, creating a critical knowledge gap concerning the longitudinal impacts on the psychosocial work environment. Further longitudinal research with larger sample sizes is needed to examine underlying sex and gender dynamics to explain why hybrid work benefits women's work experience more than men's. In addition, future research should explore diverse patterns of hybrid work (combinations of task types, office layouts, and remote intensities) and investigate why managers' work experience remained unchanged despite increased coordination demands.

#### Other relevant information
The authors emphasize a salutogenic approach focused on health-promoting workplace resources rather than solely on pathogenic risk mitigation.
