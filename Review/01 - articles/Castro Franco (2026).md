### Castro Franco (2026)

#### Reference (ABNT)
CASTRO FRANCO, Alba María. Teletrabajo y su afectación a los derechos de las personas trabajadoras desde una perspectiva de género. **IDP: Revista de Internet, Derecho y Política**, n. 44, art. 7, p. 1-16, 2026. DOI: 10.7238/idp.v0i44.425881.

#### Objective
To analyze the impact of telework on workers' rights from a gender perspective, specifically examining work-life conciliation, occupational health and safety risk prevention (ergonomic and psychosocial hazards), technology-mediated workplace harassment, and the right to digital disconnection.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
How and to what extent does telework, rather than providing a gender-neutral working environment, reproduce and exacerbate structural gender inequalities in both labor conditions and occupational risk prevention?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Qualitative legal and critical review
*   **Application:** Spanish regulatory framework, national collective agreements, labor case law, and international occupational safety standards across both private and public sector enterprises
*   **Type of work:** Remote, hybrid, and telework

#### Main findings
Telework is not a gender-neutral modality. Rather than facilitating genuine work-life balance, it frequently traps women in a double burden where professional tasks and unpaid domestic and caregiving duties are superimposed within the same domestic space. This ongoing overload acts as a severe catalyst for psychological and emotional disorders, including anxiety, depression, chronic burnout, sleep disruptions, and sedentary lifestyle. In the ergonomic sphere, women exhibit greater vulnerability to spinal and musculoskeletal disorders (cervicalgia, dorsalgia, and low back pain) due to non-neutral postures, smaller physical workstations, and gendered anthropometric mismatches. Furthermore, remote environments amplify technology-mediated violence, including digital harassment, cyberbullying, online stalking, and digital sexual harassment, which thrive in remote settings lacking direct peer oversight.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Physical, Ergonomic, Psychosocial, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** Double burden of work, work-family conflict, blurred role boundaries, high psychosocial pressure, anxiety, depression, burnout, sleep disturbances, circadian rhythm disruption, sedentary behavior, cyberbullying, digital moral harassment, technology-mediated sexual harassment, cyberstalking, doxxing, sextortion, neck pain, dorsalgia, and low back pain
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Severe psychological distress, emotional exhaustion, physical fatigue, musculoskeletal deformities, career stagnation, social isolation, and extreme fatal outcomes (such as suicide resulting from unaddressed workplace cyber harassment)

#### Risk management strategies
Execution of comprehensive, gender-sensitive workplace risk assessments that address hazards at their organizational origin. Application of gender ergonomics that adapts workstation design, display screen equipment, and physical tooling to female anthropometric and biomechanical characteristics. Establishment of a flexible communicating vessels system that enables prompt and reversible transitions between remote and on-site work to alleviate psychological strain. Implementation of technical barriers to ensure disconnection, such as automated email lockout and server-side communication blocking algorithms during non-work hours. Mandatory adoption of zero-tolerance digital harassment protocols and collective bargaining equality plans.

#### Relation to regulations
The study critically analyzes Spanish Law 10/2021 on Remote Work and Law 31/1995 on the Prevention of Occupational Risks. It evaluates Article 88 of Organic Law 3/2018 regarding the right to digital disconnection in the workplace, International Labour Organization (ILO) Convention No. 190 on Violence and Harassment (2019), and Royal Decree 901/2020 on corporate equality plans. The author criticizes these statutes for failing to establish mandatory preventive mechanisms in daily enterprise operations.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
There is an urgent need to systematically incorporate sex and gender variables into occupational health and safety data collection, risk assessment methodologies, and epidemiological investigations to visualize disguised inequalities. Collective bargaining agreements have consistently marginalized gender-responsive telework clauses, failing to establish concrete conciliation measures. Furthermore, existing legislation merely mentions digital risks without establishing practical, enforceable preventive mechanisms. Labor courts and labor inspectorates face legal uncertainty in delimiting digital harassment and proving the causal relationship between workplace conditions and employee psychological fatalities in remote contexts.

#### Other relevant information
The author discusses the landmark Iveco case in Spain, where a remote worker committed suicide following the non-consensual dissemination of intimate videos, highlighting the systemic failure of labor authorities to recognize technology-mediated harassment as a compensable work-related injury.
