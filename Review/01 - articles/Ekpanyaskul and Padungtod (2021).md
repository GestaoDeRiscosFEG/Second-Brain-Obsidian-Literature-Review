### Ekpanyaskul and Padungtod (2021)

#### Reference (ABNT)
EKPANYASKUL, Chatchai; PADUNGTOD, Chantana. Occupational health problems and lifestyle changes among novice working-from-home workers amid the COVID-19 pandemic. **Safety and Health at Work**, v. 12, n. 3, p. 384-389, 2021. DOI: 10.1016/j.shaw.2021.01.010.

#### Objective
To identify the characteristics of working from home, the magnitude of physical and psychosocial occupational health problems, and lifestyle changes among novice remote workers during the COVID-19 pandemic in Thailand, and to evaluate dose-response trends across varying extents of remote work intensity.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
What are the baseline characteristics of novice working-from-home arrangements in an urban metropolitan area? What is the prevalence of new-onset physical, ergonomic, indoor environmental, and psychosocial health problems among teleworkers? How does the intensity of weekly remote work influence the occurrence of health complaints and daily lifestyle habits?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Cross-sectional quantitative survey
*   **Application:** Web-based survey administered to 869 novice teleworkers across private and public organizations in the Bangkok metropolitan area, Thailand, during initial pandemic social distancing measures (May to June 2020)
*   **Type of work:** Work from home (telework)

#### Main findings
A high prevalence of new-onset physical and psychosocial health problems was documented. The most frequent physical health issue was weight gain (40.97%), followed by neck and shoulder pain (40.85%), low back pain (36.25%), upper extremity pain (28.42%), and eye irritation (28.19%). Among psychosocial complaints, cabin fever reached 31.28% among full-time homeworkers, alongside high rates of boundary ambiguity between work and daily life (20.6%), work anxiety (16.45%), social isolation (15.76%), loss of concentration (13.58%), work exhaustion (11.05%), and burnout (11.39%). Significant dose-response relationships (p for trend < 0.05) were identified between remote work intensity and weight changes, ergonomic discomfort, neurological symptoms, depression, and burnout. Lifestyle alterations included delayed wake-up times (61.57%), altered eating patterns (63.87%), and disrupted circadian rhythms.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial, Physical
*   **Specific risk factors:** Unsuitable domestic workstations (18.1% lacked a proper desk/chair), absence of private work rooms (37.9%), exclusive laptop use (77.5%), prolonged static sitting, poor lighting and ventilation, blurring of work-family boundaries, domestic interruptions from childcare (9.0%), and professional isolation
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Work-related musculoskeletal disorders, body weight fluctuations, visual fatigue, migraine headaches, sleep disturbances, cabin fever, chronic fatigue, depressive symptoms, burnout, and impaired workplace productivity

#### Risk management strategies
Establishing comprehensive occupational health surveillance systems adapted to remote and distributed work environments. Adopting the Total Worker Health (TWH) holistic framework to integrate physical ergonomics with psychosocial protection and chronic disease prevention. Designing guidelines to support domestic workstation setup and physical movement routines. Encouraging employers and occupational health services to prepare standardized emergency telework policies and support healthy lifestyle habits during future public health disruptions.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Structured Online Self-Administered Questionnaire]]: A 4-part electronic questionnaire developed and content-validated by five occupational medicine and psychology experts (IOC 0.8 to 1.0) measuring demographics, home office characteristics, physical/psychosocial symptoms, and lifestyle changes.
- [[Visual Analogue Scale (VAS)]]: 10-point linear scale self-administered to evaluate occupational stress levels before and during remote work.

#### Relation to regulations
not reported

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The study was conducted during the early emergency stage of the COVID-19 pandemic, capturing short-term adaptations that may underestimate cumulative chronic effects resulting from prolonged telework. The cross-sectional design precludes definitive causal verification between telework intensity and long-term health outcomes. In addition, retrospective self-assessments regarding body weight changes and physical activity may involve recall limitations, underscoring the need for prospective longitudinal studies across broader economic sectors.

#### Other relevant information
Average occupational stress scores reported during working from home (3.79 +/- 2.69 out of 10) were lower than pre-pandemic conventional office ratings (5.13 +/- 2.55).
