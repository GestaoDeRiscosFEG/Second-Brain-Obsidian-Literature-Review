### Chim and Chen (2023)

#### Reference (ABNT)
CHIM, Justine M. Y.; CHEN, Tien Li. Prediction of work from home and musculoskeletal discomfort: an investigation of ergonomic factors in work arrangements and home workstation setups using the COVID-19 experience. **International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health**, v. 20, n. 4, p. 3050, 2023. DOI: 10.3390/ijerph20043050.

#### Objective
The study aims to investigate the prevalence rate of work-related musculoskeletal disorders (MSD) among computer-based teleworkers during work from home (WFH) in Hong Kong, identify their actual work arrangements and home workstation setups, and evaluate the statistical association between ergonomic arrangements and the predicted risk of MSD.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
What is the prevalence rate of musculoskeletal problems among computer-based teleworkers during WFH in Hong Kong? What are the actual work arrangements (e.g., daily working hours, rest breaks) and physical workstation conditions utilized at home? Is there a predictable relationship between ergonomic risk factors in home furniture configurations and the development of musculoskeletal discomfort?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Cross-sectional quantitative survey
*   **Application:** Target population of 232 computer-based office workers (69% female) engaged in continuous computer tasks in Hong Kong during the COVID-19 pandemic
*   **Type of work:** Work from home (telework)

#### Main findings
A high prevalence of physical discomfort was documented, with 61.2% of homeworkers reporting musculoskeletal discomfort during WFH. Discomfort was most prominent in the neck (50.4%), upper back (45.7%), lower back (44.0%), and right shoulder (41.4%), while 42.7% reported eye strain. Constrained by limited residential living space in Hong Kong, 51.0% worked in living/dining areas and 24.6% in bedrooms, with 40.9% using dining tables and 34.1% using non-adjustable dining chairs. Over 60% relied exclusively on laptop screens. The use of a laptop monitor posed a two to three times higher risk of neck, upper back, and lower back discomfort compared to a desktop monitor. Female workers showed a significantly higher vulnerability, with an odds ratio of 2.330 for overall MSD and 2.280 for neck, lower back, and right shoulder discomfort compared to males.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial, Cognitive
*   **Specific risk factors:** Musculoskeletal discomfort, neck pain, upper and lower back pain, shoulder pain, visual fatigue, non-neutral postures, unergonomic furniture, prolonged sitting, physical and psychological fatigue, and occupational stress
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Reduced quality of life, chronic physical pain, visual fatigue, impaired mental well-being, decreased work productivity, and elevated risks of occupational illness

#### Risk management strategies
Implementation of systematic home workstation risk assessments. Employer provision of adjustable ergonomic furniture (ergonomic chairs and desks). Establishment of internal corporate reimbursement policies to subsidize home office equipment. Delivery of ergonomic health education and training. Granting worker autonomy to schedule flexible meal and rest breaks. Conducting regular organizational surveys to monitor employee physical well-being.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[MSD Assessment Tool]]: Standardized questionnaire and body mapping scale adapted from the Nordic Musculoskeletal Questionnaire to assess anatomical pain prevalence, frequency, and severity.

#### Relation to regulations
The study analyzes compliance with the Occupational Safety and Health (Display Screen Equipment) Regulation (Cap. 509B) of Hong Kong. It references European Union OSH directives and recommendations regarding display screen equipment and urges employers to formulate internal guidelines adapted to local living constraints.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Research on telework arrangements, home workstation setups, and the prevalence of musculoskeletal symptoms in densely populated metropolitan areas like Hong Kong is scarce. Optimal and practical home office setups remain underexplored in previous research. Further investigations into the feasibility and cost-benefit ratio of enterprise-sponsored home ergonomics programs are needed. Additionally, self-reported MSD data may not capture pre-existing medical conditions, and objective measurement of discomfort aggravation from unstandardized equipment remains a methodological challenge.

#### Other relevant information
The authors emphasize that home residential constraints in high-density cities require tailored OSH policies, as standard office ergonomic guidelines cannot be directly replicated in compact apartments.
