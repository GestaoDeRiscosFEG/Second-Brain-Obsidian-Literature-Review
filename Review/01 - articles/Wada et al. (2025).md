### Wada et al. (2025)

#### Reference (ABNT)
WADA, Ayane, et al. Multicomponent occupational lifestyle intervention to improve physical activity, musculoskeletal health, and work environment among Japanese teleworkers (TELEWORK study): protocol for a cluster randomized controlled trial. **Journal of Occupational Health**, v. 67, n. 1, art. uiaf014, p. 1-13, 2025. DOI: 10.1093/joccuh/uiaf014.

#### Objective
To evaluate the effectiveness of a comprehensive 12-week multicomponent occupational lifestyle intervention—integrating physical activity promotion, musculoskeletal symptom reduction, and home environmental improvements—on daily step counts, sedentary behavior, musculoskeletal pain, and work productivity among home-based Japanese teleworkers.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Does a multicomponent intervention comprising wearable activity tracking, team step competitions, ergonomic education, and environmental sensor feedback significantly increase daily step counts and reduce sedentary time in teleworkers? Does the intervention significantly alleviate neck, shoulder, and lower back discomfort, enhance home indoor air quality, and improve presenteeism compared to an assessment-only control group?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Study protocol for a two-arm, parallel-group, cluster randomized controlled trial (cRCT)
*   **Application:** Target enrollment of 240 adult teleworkers (working from home at least one day per week) clustered across eight company departments in Japan, evaluated at baseline, immediate post-intervention (week 12), and at a 12-week maintenance follow-up (week 24)
*   **Type of work:** Remote telework and hybrid office arrangements

#### Main findings
As a published trial protocol, the article presents the structural architecture and anticipated trial outcomes. The intervention hypothesizes that combining individual behavioral feedback (wearable activity trackers and micro-break notifications), social support mechanisms (team-based corporate step competitions), tele-health coaching sessions, and real-time indoor environmental sensor alerts (CO2 and temperature tracking) will produce sustained increases in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity and substantial reductions in uninterrupted sitting time. Expected secondary outcomes include significant reductions in the severity and frequency of neck, shoulder, and low back pain, enhanced sleep quality, lowered psychosocial distress, and measurable reductions in sickness presenteeism.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Physical, Psychosocial
*   **Specific risk factors:** Prolonged uninterrupted occupational sitting, loss of incidental physical mobility, awkward posture from inadequate home desks, elevated indoor carbon dioxide (CO2) concentrations from poor ventilation, lighting glare, high work-related psychological stress, and digital isolation
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Work-related musculoskeletal disorders, chronic neck and shoulder stiffness, acute lumbar strain, visual fatigue, impaired sleep hygiene, presenteeism, and elevated long-term cardio-metabolic health risks

#### Risk management strategies
Deployment of triaxial accelerometers to provide continuous objective step and sitting feedback. Execution of web-based team step challenges to leverage peer motivation and corporate social support. Conducting structured virtual group health guidance sessions led by certified occupational health consultants. Installing continuous indoor air quality sensors to prompt window ventilation when indoor CO2 exceeds healthy thresholds. Disseminating educational infographics on home office ergonomics, micro-break scheduling, and daily stretching routines.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Active-style Pro HJA-350IT]]: A calibrated triaxial accelerometer worn at the waist to objectively measure daily step counts, sedentary time (<1.5 METs), and physical activity intensity.
- [[Q-Umon IAQ Sensor]]: An Internet of Things environmental monitoring station continuously measuring temperature, relative humidity, and indoor carbon dioxide (CO2) levels in teleworkers' domestic workrooms.
- [[Brief Job Stress Questionnaire (BJSQ)]]: Standardized 57-item Japanese occupational health tool measuring job stressors, psychological distress, and workplace social support.
- [[WPAI]]: Work Productivity and Activity Impairment questionnaire assessing absenteeism and presenteeism.
- [[Nordic Musculoskeletal Questionnaire (NMQ)]]: Validated instrument evaluating musculoskeletal discomfort prevalence across nine anatomical body regions.

#### Relation to regulations
The protocol is aligned with the Japanese Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) Guidelines for Promoting Appropriate Teleworking (2021) and statutory guidelines on occupational hygiene in computer display work. The study protocol received institutional ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of the Physical Education and Medicine Research Center (Approval No. 2023-03).

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
To date, no comprehensive intervention studies simultaneously address physical activity, musculoskeletal ergonomics, and indoor environmental factors among home-based teleworkers. Previous workplace lifestyle interventions predominantly targeted single risk factors in centralized physical corporate offices. The authors emphasize the critical need for cluster-randomized trials that combine wearable objective biosensors with organizational peer support to establish scalable, evidence-based OSH practices in post-pandemic telework settings.

#### Other relevant information
The trial features a 12-week post-intervention follow-up to test whether remote workers sustain physical activity habits after external coaching and team competitions cease.
