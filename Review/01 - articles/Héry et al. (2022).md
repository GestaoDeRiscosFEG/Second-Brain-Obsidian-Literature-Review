### Héry et al. (2022)

#### Reference (ABNT)
HÉRY, Michel, et al. Medium-term consequences (5 years) of the Covid-19 crisis on work organization and occupational risks: a prospective study. **European Journal of Futures Research**, v. 10, n. 1, art. 11, p. 1-13, 2022. DOI: 10.1186/s40309-022-00197-4.

#### Objective
To provide enterprises and public policy bodies with strategic foresight elements to navigate the medium-term transformations (up to a 5-year horizon, 2025) in work organization accelerated by the health crisis, and to anticipate emerging occupational safety and health risks associated with the hybridization of work.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Will organizational transformations triggered by the pandemic prove durable over the medium term? What consequences will these structural evolutions have on work organization, working conditions, and occupational risk prevention over a 5-year horizon?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Strategic foresight qualitative prospective study using morphological analysis and contrasting scenario construction
*   **Application:** Expert panel organized by the French National Research and Safety Institute (INRS) comprising 17 OSH researchers and 13 corporate executives from eight major French enterprises (exceeding 2,000 employees) across manufacturing, transport, logistics, and public administration
*   **Type of work:** Remote telework, on-site industrial work, and platform gig work

#### Main findings
The health crisis acted as an intense accelerator of pre-existing organizational trends: rapid production automation, expansion of algorithmic performance management, contract casualization, and the hybridization of conventional enterprises with digital platform business models. Synthesis of contrasting scenarios identified five pivotal determinants of occupational risks over a five-year horizon: (1) technology adoption for collaboration and automation; (2) work organization flexibility and task scheduling; (3) worker contractual status; (4) collective worker bargaining and the sustainability of work collectives; and (5) the control of organizational interfaces multiplied by task fragmentation. Accelerated hybrid work without institutional risk assessment creates organizational gray zones where traditional employer safety obligations are obscured, leading to an anticipated surge in work-related accidents, musculoskeletal disorders from improvised home settings, and severe mental exhaustion resulting from blurred boundaries and social isolation.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Psychosocial, Ergonomic, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** High cognitive stress, mental overload from blurred boundaries between professional and domestic spheres, social and professional isolation (particularly among young and newly hired teleworkers), self-exploitation, loss of work meaning, work-life conflict, musculoskeletal disorders from non-standardized home desks, task fragmentation, and loss of control over multiplied work interfaces
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Chronic psychosocial stress, depressive disorders, deterioration of collective solidarity, work accidents due to poorly managed subcontracting interfaces, and cumulative musculoskeletal tissue damage

#### Risk management strategies
Embedding strategic foresight and morphological scenario analysis into corporate prevention policies to anticipate emerging hazards. Actively managing organizational interfaces and interdependencies among contractors, subcontractors, and teleworkers to prevent accountability vacuums. Rebuilding and supporting sustainable work collectives to overcome remote isolation and preserve informal professional knowledge sharing. Establishing negotiated corporate frameworks on telework that set explicit availability limits and guarantee ergonomic equipment provision.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Morphological Analysis]]: A systematic strategic foresight method used to map permutations of key organizational uncertainty variables (telework role, work collectives, labor relations, automation) to build four contrasting future scenarios.
- [[Contrasting Scenarios Method]]: INRS qualitative framework generating four contrasting future narratives to anticipate emerging OSH risks and formulate adaptive organizational prevention strategies.

#### Relation to regulations
The study analyzes the erosion of conventional legal employment contracts and the proliferation of commercial service and gig-platform contracts. The authors highlight the lag in statutory labor regulations to cover remote working gray zones and advocate for collective bargaining agreements to define the boundaries of remote work.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
No previous prospective studies systematically explored post-pandemic labor reorganizations with a specific focus on occupational risk prevention. The study notes limitations regarding its broad inter-sectoral scope, which could not explore micro-level sectoral specificities in depth. The 5-year time horizon may not fully capture radical technological disruptions beyond 2025. The authors highlight the need for continuous bibliographic and field monitoring to track the real-world trajectory of work hybridization and emerging mental health hazards.

#### Other relevant information
The authors emphasize that the most hazardous consequence of remote work hybridization is the dismantling of informal collective support networks among peers.
