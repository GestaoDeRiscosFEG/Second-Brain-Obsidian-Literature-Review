### Iordache et al. (2025)

#### Reference (ABNT)
IORDACHE, Raluca Maria; CIOCA, Victoria-Rodica; MIHAILA, Delia. Digitalization in the world of work - benefits and new risks for employees' mental health. **Polish Journal of Management Studies**, v. 32, n. 2, p. 111-124, 2025. DOI: 10.17512/pjms.2025.32.2.07.

#### Objective
This study aims to systematically examine the dual impact of workplace digitalization on employees' mental health, evaluate the limitations of existing OHS frameworks in addressing these novel hazards, and identify evidence-based organizational and regulatory interventions that preserve the advantages of digital transformation while protecting worker well-being.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
What are the primary mental health benefits and significant psychosocial risks associated with contemporary workplace digitalization? To what extent do existing OHS frameworks identify and mitigate digitalization-related mental health risks? Which organizational interventions and policy measures show promise in reducing negative mental health impacts while preserving digital transformation benefits?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Narrative literature review, qualitative
*   **Application:** General work context involving computer-mediated and digital work, not limited to office or technology sectors
*   **Type of work:** Remote and hybrid work

#### Main findings
Digitalization provides substantial benefits including greater flexibility, autonomy, streamlined communication, cross-departmental collaboration, and increased access to digital mental health support tools. However, it also introduces and intensifies severe psychosocial risks, including constant connectivity under an always-on culture, boundary erosion, technostress, digital fatigue, cognitive overload, job insecurity from AI automation, loss of autonomy under algorithmic management, and social isolation. Traditional OHS frameworks fail to identify or mitigate these digital hazards due to the absence of validated metrics for digital demands. Comprehensive, organization-wide interventions such as enforceable right-to-disconnect policies, boundary-management training, and integrated OHS updates demonstrate greater effectiveness than isolated individual wellness initiatives.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Psychosocial, Cognitive, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** Stress, burnout, anxiety, depression, digital fatigue, constant connectivity, work-life boundary erosion, work-family conflict, technostress, job insecurity, loss of autonomy, algorithmic surveillance, social isolation, cyberchondria, and digital literacy disparities
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Impaired employee mental health, chronic stress, emotional exhaustion, psychological distress, decreased life satisfaction, reduced job performance, and substantial global economic productivity losses

#### Risk management strategies
Implementation of right-to-disconnect policies and explicit boundary-setting norms regarding after-hours availability. Development of training programs focused on digital well-being, boundary management, and leadership mental health literacy. Redesign of systems and tools to minimize invasive monitoring, integrate mental health into performance appraisal, and deploy supportive technologies. Creation of social and cultural initiatives such as structured virtual interactions, peer-support networks, and stigma-reduction campaigns. Establishment of integrated OHS updates requiring mandatory inclusion of digital and AI-related risks in workplace risk assessments.

#### Relation to regulations
The study critically evaluates the ISO 45003 standard and the UK Health and Safety Executive (HSE) Management Standards, noting that their analog design fails to capture digital hazards such as algorithmic surveillance and expected response times. It reviews statutory right-to-disconnect legislation implemented in jurisdictions such as France, Belgium, Portugal, and Spain, noting substantial reductions in after-hours communication and emotional exhaustion. It emphasizes that organizations should serve as primary regulatory agents in establishing internal protective policies.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Traditional OHS risk assessment models developed for physical and classical psychosocial hazards are largely incapable of detecting or managing risks specific to digital environments and artificial intelligence. There is a need for longitudinal cohort studies measuring mental health outcomes before and after implementing right-to-disconnect policies or redesigned algorithmic systems. Further research must develop and internationally validate standardized digital psychosocial risk assessment instruments suitable for regulatory compliance. Comparative investigations are required to examine differential exposure between permanent and precarious workers, age groups, and levels of digital literacy. Randomized controlled trials should test bundled organizational interventions combining policy reform, boundary training, surveillance reduction, and structured social connection. Research should also explore cultural and sectoral variations in technostress and boundary blurring.

#### Other relevant information
The study highlights artificial intelligence and algorithmic monitoring as supervisory surveillance mechanisms over remote workers, suggesting the relevance of surveying employee exposure to AI-driven monitoring software.
