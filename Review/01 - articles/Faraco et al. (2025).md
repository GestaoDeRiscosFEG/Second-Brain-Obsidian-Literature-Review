### Faraco et al. (2025)

#### Reference (ABNT)
FARACO, Raquel Malheiros; BROBERG, Ole; DUARTE, Francisco José de Castro Moura. The impact of COVID-19 on support teams. **Work: A Journal of Prevention, Assessment and Rehabilitation**, v. 80, n. 2, p. 622-642, 2025. DOI: 10.3233/WOR-230565.

#### Objective
To investigate the impacts and organizational learning of remote work on safety knowledge management within specialized onshore teams that support offshore drilling rig operations during the COVID-19 pandemic, using Ergonomic Work Analysis (EWA) and the Work System Model (WSM).

#### Research questions or hypotheses
How was the original support team work system structured before the pandemic? What direct changes in the work system were provoked by the sudden shift to remote work? How did geographic dispersion and remote collaboration affect daily work activities, tacit safety knowledge sharing, and overall service delivery? What individual and collective strategies were devised by workers to cope with the loss of in-person interactions?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Longitudinal qualitative case study utilizing Ergonomic Work Analysis (EWA) and the Work System Model (WSM)
*   **Application:** In-depth investigation of an onshore operational support service in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, comprising 60 multidisciplinary technical experts rotating in 12-hour shifts supporting approximately 20 offshore drilling rigs, based on pre-pandemic in-situ observations and 117 semi-structured interviews during and after the pandemic
*   **Type of work:** Remote telework from home, decentralized satellite offices, and on-site collocated operations

#### Main findings
The emergency transition to remote telework altered team interactions, drastically reducing spontaneous and informal communications. The loss of face-to-face contact weakened interpersonal trust and hindered the transfer of tacit safety knowledge, which is vital for anticipating complex, non-routine drilling anomalies. Remote work created communication friction, information overload across fragmented chat groups, and fatigue from continuous virtual meetings. Conversely, isolated home telework significantly improved individual performance on asynchronous tasks (such as operational data review, test follow-up, and report generation) by eliminating office interruptions and enhancing focus. To overcome remote friction, experts developed resilient collective coping practices, notably mandatory daily virtual shift alignment meetings and structured handover protocols, which proved so effective that they were permanently retained upon returning to the physical office.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Psychosocial, Organizational, Ergonomic
*   **Specific risk factors:** Social and professional isolation, loss of hallway spontaneous interactions, barriers to tacit knowledge articulation, high cognitive workload from tracking multi-channel chats, back-to-back video call fatigue, and deficient domestic physical workstations
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Weakened team cohesion, loss of collective situational awareness, heightened cognitive exhaustion, blurred work-life boundaries, and potential latency in anticipating critical industrial hazards

#### Risk management strategies
Institutionalizing daily 15-to-30-minute virtual alignment meetings at the beginning of shifts to establish shared operational understanding. Structuring standardized team chat channels with designated subject tags to prevent information fragmentation. Establishing explicit boundaries on online meeting durations and participant lists to prevent meeting overload. Providing flexibility regarding webcam usage to protect domestic intimacy and accommodate unstable residential internet bandwidth. Supplying ergonomic furniture and IT infrastructure for home workstations.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Ergonomic Work Analysis (EWA)]]: Methodological framework following Guérin et al. (2007) to observe, analyze, and diagnose real work activity versus prescribed tasks across five iterative stages.
- [[Work System Model (WSM)]]: Conceptual framework based on Alter (2006) used to systematize data across nine interrelated work system elements (customers, products, processes, participants, information, technologies, environment, infrastructure, and strategies).

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The Ergonomics and Human Factors (E/HF) pandemic literature predominantly focused on healthcare environments, leaving safety-critical industrial support activities underexplored. The study notes that while asynchronous tasks thrived in remote setups, the long-term impact of remote dispersion on collective anticipation of rare, high-severity operational anomalies remains difficult to measure quantitatively without longitudinal incident tracking. Furthermore, the findings reflect a highly specialized engineering cohort, warranting comparative research across other high-reliability industries (such as nuclear and chemical processing).

#### Other relevant information
The study documents how tools initially introduced as emergency workarounds (such as Microsoft Teams) became central pillars of organizational knowledge governance.
