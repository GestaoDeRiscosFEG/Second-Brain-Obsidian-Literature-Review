### Erikson and Rosin (2025)

#### Reference (ABNT)
ERIKSON, Merle; ROSIN, Annika. Protection of the occupational health and safety of teleworkers. **European Labour Law Journal**, v. 16, n. 2, p. 228-244, 2025.

#### Objective
The article aims to examine what the occupational safety and health (OSH) obligations of the employer and employee should be in the case of telework. It carries out a comparative legal analysis of Estonian and Finnish labour legislation in the broader context of European Union employment law, evaluating whether current OSH rules remain suitable in light of the practical and constitutional constraints of remote work.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
How the employer can ensure healthy and safe working conditions for the teleworker. What the OSH obligations of the employer and employee should be in the case of telework.

#### Method
*   **Type:** Qualitative legal analysis
*   **Application:** Telework, home-office
*   **Type of work:** Remote

#### Main findings
Because the employee exercises greater autonomy than usual in organizing telework, compliance with OSH rules requires a foundation of trust rather than direct hierarchical control between the parties to the employment contract. Consequently, the employer must verify that the employee possesses the experience and skills necessary to care for their own safety and health. If the employer is not convinced, they may refrain from concluding the telework agreement and require work to be performed on company premises. The study does not present empirical metrics, as it is a qualitative analysis examining the legal distribution of statutory obligations.

*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial
*   **Specific risk factors:** Musculoskeletal pain, mental workload, psychosocial factors

#### Risk management strategies
Employers may conduct risk assessments through questionnaires or interviews to evaluate working conditions when physical access to the home is restricted. Employers can also assess general hazards foreseeable in telework, such as improper posture and poor lighting. Furthermore, organizations should provide internal training and guidance to ensure workers recognize hazards and understand preventive measures. Under European Union labor provisions, employers must also supply comprehensive information regarding company safety policies and OSH measures.

#### Relation to regulations
The study analyzes European Union legislation, specifically Council Directive 89/391/EEC (Framework Directive) and the 2002 European Framework Agreement on Telework, comparing them with national statutory frameworks in Finland (Occupational Safety and Health Act, Section 5) and Estonia (Occupational Health and Safety Act - TTOS, Sections 12 and 135). The authors examine how Finnish law permits derogations from specific employer duties when influence over working conditions is limited, whereas Estonian law maintains a mandatory core of non-derogable duties while allowing flexibility for other obligations.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Although the Framework Directive and the Telework Agreement mandate that teleworkers receive equal OSH protection, complete enforcement of these rules in telework is impossible in practice, making it unreasonable to require employers to satisfy duties that cannot be executed. Because the Framework Directive was adopted decades before telework became widespread, consideration should be given to amending its principles. In addition, while employer obligations are practically reduced, employee responsibility for OSH inevitably increases in telework. Finnish law admits this practical outcome without altering the party primarily responsible for OSH, whereas Estonian law explicitly provides that the employee is primarily responsible for arranging a safe workplace, creating tension regarding how responsibilities should be balanced between the parties. Finally, the analysis focused on telework in fixed home locations and public spaces, leaving other remote configurations for further examination.

#### Other relevant information
Summary of employer and employee duties in telework arrangements, emphasizing mutual trust and worker autonomy.
