### Cropley et al. (2023)

#### Reference (ABNT)
CROPLEY, Mark, et al. Working from home during lockdown: the association between rest breaks and well-being. **Ergonomics**, v. 66, n. 4, p. 443-453, 2023. DOI: 10.1080/00140139.2022.2095038.

#### Objective
To investigate the association between internal recovery—operationalized as rest break frequency (low, medium, and high) during the workday—and self-reported musculoskeletal pain symptoms as well as post-work psychological recovery outcomes among home-based knowledge workers during lockdown.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
How does the frequency of internal recovery rest breaks during working from home influence musculoskeletal pain symptoms (headaches, neck, lower back, arm)? Does failing to take regular rest breaks impair post-work recovery indicators, including psychological detachment, physical fatigue, mental fatigue, and sleep quality?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Cross-sectional quantitative survey
*   **Application:** Online questionnaire completed by 382 knowledge workers in the United Kingdom working remotely from home for 25 hours or more per week during national pandemic lockdown (November 2020 to February 2021)
*   **Type of work:** Work from home (telework)

#### Main findings
Failing to take regular rest breaks during the teleworking day was associated with a statistically significant, dose-response increased risk of reporting headaches: workers in the low-break frequency group were more than twice as likely to suffer from headaches compared to frequent-break takers (adjusted OR = 2.16, 95% CI: 1.13 to 4.13), even after adjusting for age, gender, working hours, job autonomy, and job stress. Regarding post-work recovery, low break frequency was dramatically associated with an increased risk of mental fatigue (adjusted OR = 5.62), inadequate rest (adjusted OR = 3.22), poor sleep (adjusted OR = 3.22), physical fatigue (crude OR = 2.80), and impaired psychological detachment from work (crude OR = 2.46). In the absence of structured office routines and informal social watercooler breaks, home teleworkers frequently neglected rest pauses, accelerating cognitive and physical exhaustion.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** Infrequent rest breaks, prolonged uninterrupted static sitting, continuous display screen exposure, blurred boundaries between domestic and professional domains, after-hours work rumination, and digital presenteeism
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Frequent tension headaches, severe mental and physical fatigue, acute insomnia, sleep disruption, chronic inability to detach psychologically from job tasks, and cumulative stress

#### Risk management strategies
Educating teleworkers and corporate managers on the biological value of taking regular, short micro-breaks throughout the working day. Encouraging workers to formulate explicit daily intentions regarding pause scheduling. Implementing an empowering, trusting leadership style that delegates autonomy and avoids invasive digital surveillance. Providing guidance on boundary management to ensure psychological detachment during evenings. Supplying adjustable ergonomic home workstation equipment to alleviate physical load.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Work-Related Rumination Questionnaire (WRRQ)]]: Psychometric instrument measuring affective rumination and inability to achieve psychological detachment from work.
- [[Rest Break Frequency Scale]]: Single-item categorical assessment operationalizing internal recovery frequency into low (seldom/never), medium (sometimes), and high (regularly/frequently) tiers.

not reported

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The cross-sectional design precludes definitive causal determinations regarding the directionality between rest pauses and well-being. Survey timing early during lockdown may not have provided sufficient duration for chronic spinal musculoskeletal disorders to fully manifest. The study measured break frequency but did not capture break duration or the specific nature of break activities (e.g., active walking vs. passive sedentary screen scrolling). Furthermore, findings from forced pandemic teleworking may not fully generalize to voluntary post-pandemic hybrid routines.

#### Other relevant information
The authors highlight the shift in responsibility for the physical and temporal work environment from the employer to the remote employee, urging organizations to support worker self-regulation.
