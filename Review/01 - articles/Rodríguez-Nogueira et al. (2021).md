### Rodríguez-Nogueira et al. (2021)

#### Reference (ABNT)
RODRÍGUEZ-NOGUEIRA, Óscar, et al. Musculoskeletal pain and teleworking in times of the COVID-19: analysis of the impact on the workers at two Spanish universities. **International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health**, v. 18, n. 1, art. 31, p. 1-12, 2021. DOI: 10.3390/ijerph18010031.

#### Objective
To analyze the impact of mandatory teleworking during COVID-19 home confinement on musculoskeletal pain, perceived stress, and daily physical activity patterns among university staff, and to identify demographic and behavioral variables predicting changes in pain prevalence.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Did mandatory home teleworking during lockdown alter the prevalence and anatomical distribution of musculoskeletal pain among university employees? How did self-selected physical activity routines and sedentary durations interact with musculoskeletal discomfort and perceived stress during remote work?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Cross-sectional observational quantitative survey
*   **Application:** Web-based questionnaire administered to 472 administrative and teaching staff across two public universities in Spain (University of León and University of Salamanca) during national lockdown confinement
*   **Type of work:** Telework from home

#### Main findings
Contrary to initial assumptions, the prevalence of musculoskeletal pain across all anatomical regions decreased significantly during home teleworking compared to pre-lockdown office work (from 84.5% to 69.5%, p < 0.001), with the most pronounced reductions observed in the lower back (46.4% to 33.1%), neck (51.5% to 38.6%), and upper back (39.0% to 26.5%). Daily sedentary sitting time increased from 6.8 to 8.2 hours (p < 0.001); however, workers simultaneously engaged in more frequent and varied physical exercise at home (particularly strength training, core exercises, and stretching). Multivariate logistic regression revealed that biological sex was the single most robust independent predictor of musculoskeletal pain during teleworking: female workers were nearly three times more likely to report pain than male colleagues (OR = 2.76, 95% CI: 1.76 to 4.33). Autonomy in scheduling flexible physical activity breaks during the day served as an effective personal buffer against pain exacerbation.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial
*   **Specific risk factors:** Prolonged sitting time, improvised residential furniture, laptop monitor misalignment, domestic multi-tasking, blurred boundaries, and occupational stress
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Work-related musculoskeletal disorders, persistent cervicalgia, dorsalgia, lumbar backache, physical fatigue, and elevated psychological stress

#### Risk management strategies
Promoting autonomous, self-chosen physical activity routines (strength training and stretching exercises) integrated into the teleworking day. Granting workers schedule autonomy to distribute rest breaks at preferred intervals. Distributing guidance on home workstation ergonomic alignment. Incorporating gender-specific ergonomic risk assessments that account for the significantly higher vulnerability of female teleworkers to musculoskeletal strain.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Nordic Musculoskeletal Questionnaire (NMQ)]]: Standardized self-administered survey assessing musculoskeletal discomfort across nine anatomical regions before and during telework.
- [[Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-14)]]: 14-item instrument evaluating subjective psychological stress levels during the confinement period.
- [[IPAQ-SF]]: International Physical Activity Questionnaire - Short Form measuring weekly physical activity intensity and daily sitting hours.

#### Relation to regulations
The study discusses compliance with Spanish Royal Decree 463/2020 declaring the national state of alarm and Royal Decree-Law 8/2020 establishing teleworking as the mandatory preferential mode of employment, emphasizing that occupational risk prevention services faced severe operational constraints during emergency transitions.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The study relied on self-reported questionnaires for physical activity, pain, and stress rather than objective biomechanical measurements or expert clinical assessments (e.g., using accelerometers, inclinometers, or physician evaluations). The cross-sectional design cannot verify temporal causality or eliminate recall bias. Furthermore, because the sample was recruited exclusively from two Spanish universities, findings cannot be directly generalized to non-academic sectors or differing occupational demographics. Future research should evaluate whether sustained employee-chosen exercise habits maintain pain reductions in post-pandemic hybrid settings.

#### Other relevant information
The authors emphasize that when workers gain autonomy over their daily schedules, they voluntarily adopt protective physical activities that counteract the adverse biomechanical effects of non-standard home furniture.
