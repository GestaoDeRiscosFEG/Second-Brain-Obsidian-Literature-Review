### Niedhammer et al. (2024)

#### Reference (ABNT)
NIEDHAMMER, Isabelle; PINEAU, Eve; ROSANKIS, Elodie. The associations of psychosocial work exposures with suicidal ideation in the national French SUMER study. **Journal of Affective Disorders**, v. 356, p. 699-706, 2024. DOI: 10.1016/j.jad.2024.04.070.

#### Objective
To examine the associations between a comprehensive set of occupational exposures—specifically psychosocial work exposures—and suicidal ideation in a large, nationally representative sample of French salaried workers, evaluating differences across genders and controlling for physical, chemical, and biomechanical occupational factors.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
What are the associations between occupational exposures, particularly psychosocial work factors, and suicidal ideation in a representative working population? Do psychosocial work hazards (such as job strain, effort-reward imbalance, workplace bullying, work-family conflict, ethical conflict, and telework) independently elevate the risk of suicidal ideation, and do physical or chemical hazards show similar associations after adjusting for covariates?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Cross-sectional epidemiological survey
*   **Application:** National survey based on the French SUMER 2010 database, encompassing a representative sample of 25,977 employees (14,682 men and 11,295 women) assessed through paired occupational physician medical evaluations and self-administered questionnaires across services (80.3%), industry (12.9%), construction (5.6%), and agriculture (1.3%)
*   **Type of work:** On-site conventional work and telework (at least 1 day per week, representing 3.09% of the sample)

#### Main findings
The 12-month prevalence of suicidal ideation was 3.5%, with no significant difference between men and women. Psychosocial work exposures were the primary occupational determinants of suicidal ideation. Workplace bullying exhibited the strongest association with suicidal ideation among both men (OR = 3.32) and women (OR = 2.40). Crucially, telework (working remotely at least one day per week) was identified as an independent risk factor associated with significantly elevated odds of suicidal ideation in the fully adjusted final multivariable model (OR = 1.77, 95% CI: 1.07 to 2.94). Additional independent psychosocial risk factors included low recognition/reward, high psychological demands, work-family conflict, low social support, ethical conflict, and low meaning of work. Conversely, physical, biomechanical, chemical, and biological workplace exposures showed no significant association with suicidal ideation once adjusted for psychosocial factors and covariates.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Psychosocial, Organizational, Physical
*   **Specific risk factors:** Workplace moral harassment (bullying), psychological job strain (high demands combined with low decision latitude), low supervisor and coworker support, effort-reward imbalance, job insecurity, work-family conflict, ethical conflict, loss of meaning of work, and telework-induced isolation
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Suicidal ideation, clinical depression, severe psychological distress, absenteeism, and long-term disability leave

#### Risk management strategies
Designing and enforcing comprehensive enterprise anti-bullying and anti-harassment policies with clear reporting and mediation procedures. Systematic assessment and monitoring of psychosocial work factors as part of mandatory occupational health surveillance. Re-evaluating work design to enhance employee decision latitude, recognition, and social support. Implementing targeted mental health safeguards, communication routines, and social support mechanisms for teleworkers to counteract isolation and boundary friction.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[PHQ-9]]: Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (specifically Item 9) assessing the presence and frequency of suicidal ideation within the previous 14 days.
- [[Job Content Questionnaire (JCQ)]]: Karasek's validated instrument measuring psychological job demands, decision latitude, and workplace social support.
- [[ERI Questionnaire]]: Siegrist's Effort-Reward Imbalance scale measuring occupational rewards (esteem, promotion, and job security).
- [[SUMER Survey Questionnaire]]: Standardized French national survey protocol combining clinical occupational physician interviews and employee self-administered questionnaires.

#### Relation to regulations
The study discusses French labor codes, the institutional role of occupational medicine services (*services de santé au travail*), and compares regulatory frameworks on working hours and worker mental health protections against international benchmarks.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Scientific evidence regarding workplace risk factors for suicidal behavior remains limited, with researchers noting that this field is still relatively young. The cross-sectional survey design precludes definitive causal conclusions, as reverse causality (where depressive symptoms influence reporting of job strain) cannot be ruled out. In addition, reporting bias and a healthy worker survivor effect (severely impaired individuals leaving the labor market) may underestimate associations. Certain organizational constructs, such as organizational injustice, were not captured in the survey. Further longitudinal research is warranted to track teleworking cohorts and examine suicidal behavior trajectories over time.

#### Other relevant information
The authors emphasize that suicide prevention strategies should not focus solely on individual psychiatric vulnerabilities, but must recognize and address organizational and psychosocial work conditions as actionable occupational health hazards.
