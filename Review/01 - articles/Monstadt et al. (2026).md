### Monstadt et al. (2026)

#### Reference (ABNT)
MONSTADT, Andrea, et al. Multidomain correlates of burnout: a population-based study using supervised machine learning. **Social Psychiatry and Psychiatric Epidemiology**, v. 61, p. 1-13, 2026. DOI: 10.1007/s00127-025-02844-3.

#### Objective
To determine whether and how burnout correlates with diverse organizational factors, psychosocial employment conditions, sociodemographic variables, and mental health indicators in a large population-based cohort, and to identify the most significant predictors of burnout severity using supervised machine learning.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
What are the relative contributions and relative importance of multidomain variables in explaining burnout variance? Do nonlinear patterns and feature interactions exist among organizational, physical, and psychological correlates? To what extent do psychosocial work conditions and clinical mental health parameters outweigh traditional sociodemographic and physical factors in predicting emotional exhaustion?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Population-based cross-sectional survey analyzed with supervised machine learning algorithms (Random Forest and Gradient Boosted Trees with SHAP explainability)
*   **Application:** Baseline assessment of the German National Cohort (NAKO) encompassing 27,331 employed individuals across 18 regional study centers in Germany
*   **Type of work:** Remote, on-site, and flexible work across diverse industries

#### Main findings
Supervised machine learning algorithms explained approximately 48% of the variance in burnout severity. Psychosocial work characteristics and clinical mental health status emerged as the dominant predictors of burnout. Specifically, effort-reward imbalance (ERI ratio), high overcommitment, depressive symptoms (PHQ-9), and generalized anxiety (GAD-7) exhibited the strongest predictive importance. Sociodemographic variables (such as age and gender), employment contracts, and physical working environment parameters had negligible relative contributions compared to psychosocial factors. The analysis demonstrated reciprocal dynamics, where mental health symptoms and perceived lack of occupational recognition act as mutually reinforcing drivers of exhaustion.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Psychosocial, Organizational, Physical
*   **Specific risk factors:** High job effort, lack of occupational rewards (inadequate pay, low esteem, poor promotional prospects), work overload, psychological overcommitment, social isolation, excessive display screen exposure, and musculoskeletal discomfort
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Clinical depression, chronic anxiety disorders, musculoskeletal pain, job dissatisfaction, elevated turnover intentions, absenteeism, and long-term disability leave

#### Risk management strategies
Designing and implementing leadership management training programs focused on mitigating effort-reward imbalances. Fostering managerial behaviors that provide regular professional appreciation, career advancement clarity, and transparent organizational communication. Establishing confidential clinical psychological counseling services and preventive mental health screening within workplace health programs. Redesigning task allocations to prevent chronic overcommitment and structural overtime.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[MBI-GS]]: The Maslach Burnout Inventory - General Survey, utilizing the 5-item emotional exhaustion subscale to measure core burnout severity.
- [[ERI Questionnaire]]: Siegrist's Effort-Reward Imbalance Questionnaire measuring extrinsic effort, occupational rewards (esteem, financial, career opportunities), and intrinsic overcommitment.
- [[PHQ-9]]: Patient Health Questionnaire-9 evaluating depressive symptom severity.
- [[GAD-7]]: Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 screening tool assessing generalized anxiety symptoms.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
The cross-sectional design of the baseline cohort precludes establishing temporal precedence and definitive causal trajectories between psychosocial exposures, depressive symptoms, and burnout progression. In addition, reliance on self-report instruments may introduce subjective reporting bias and common method variance. The authors emphasize that future research should utilize prospective longitudinal panels and investigate sector-specific intervention trials to evaluate organizational reward-restructuring initiatives.

#### Other relevant information
The study highlights that interventions solely targeting individual resilience are insufficient if systemic organizational imbalances between effort and recognition are not resolved.
