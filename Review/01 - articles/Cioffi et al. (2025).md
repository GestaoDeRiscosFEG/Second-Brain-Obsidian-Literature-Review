### Cioffi et al. (2025)

#### Reference (ABNT)
CIOFFI, Glauco; BALDUCCI, Cristian; TODERI, Stefano. Digital stress-preventive management competencies: definition, identification and tool development for research and practice. **International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health**, v. 22, n. 2, art. 267, p. 1-26, 2025. DOI: 10.3390/ijerph22020267.

#### Objective
To conceptualize and define Digital Stress-Preventive Management Competencies (DMCs) for supervisors managing remote workers and to develop and validate a practical indicator tool (DMCIT) through a three-phase mixed-methods procedure assessing its relationship with psychosocial working conditions.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Which supervisory behaviors optimize the psychosocial work environment and well-being of remote workers? What is the factor structure, reliability, and validity of the new Digital Management Competencies Indicator Tool? How does manager-team agreement or disagreement on digital competencies influence subordinate perceptions of supervisor support, role clarity, demands, and job control?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Three-phase mixed-methods study (qualitative exploratory development, quantitative psychometric validation, and multi-source concurrent validity analysis)
*   **Application:** Hybrid and remote employees and supervisors across Italian public administrations engaged in organizational well-being interventions
*   **Type of work:** Remote and hybrid work

#### Main findings
The study identified two core supervisory competencies: Supportive ICT-Mediated Interaction (SIMI, 5 items) and Avoidance of Abusive ICT Adoption (AAIA, 4 items). Confirmatory factor analysis supported the two-factor structure of the 9-item tool (DMCIT) with sound psychometric fit. Structural equation modeling demonstrated that SIMI is significantly and positively associated with superior support and role clarity, whereas AAIA is positively linked to job control and negatively associated with perceived job demands. Polynomial regressions and response surface analysis indicated that manager-team agreement on digital competencies fostered role clarity and job control, with teams supervised by managers who underestimated their own skills reporting more favorable psychosocial working conditions than those led by overestimating managers.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Psychosocial, Organizational, Cognitive
*   **Specific risk factors:** Uncontrolled digital ubiquity, after-hours work communication, invasive electronic monitoring, cognitive role ambiguity, high psychological demands, technostress, and work-life boundary erosion
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Work-related stress, emotional exhaustion, reduced job autonomy, sleep disturbances, and impaired psychological well-being

#### Risk management strategies
Adopting explicit communication guidelines to select appropriate channels between face-to-face and virtual media. Establishing prompt feedback mechanisms and scheduled updates for remote team members. Respecting employee work-life boundaries by strictly avoiding non-emergency emails and calls during vacations, sick leaves, and outside working hours. Eliminating invasive micro-monitoring behaviors to support employee autonomy. Using the DMCIT instrument for upward feedback, managerial self-reflection, and diagnostic training needs assessment.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[DMCIT]]: The Digital Management Competencies Indicator Tool, a 9-item validated scale measuring two dimensions (Supportive ICT-Mediated Interaction and Avoidance of Abusive ICT Adoption) on a 5-point Likert response scale.
- [[Management Standards Indicator Tool]]: Validated questionnaire adapted from the UK HSE framework to assess supervisor support, role, demands, and control.

#### Relation to regulations
The study grounds its conceptual framework in the United Nations 2030 Agenda (SDG 3 and SDG 8) and aligns with international statutory developments regarding the right to digital disconnection enacted in jurisdictions such as France, Italy, Spain, Belgium, Germany, and Australia.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Extant digital leadership frameworks predominantly focus on performance optimization in educational or technology sectors, overlooking supervisor competencies geared specifically toward occupational health and psychosocial stress prevention. The study notes limitations regarding cross-sectional field designs, modest sample sizes within public administration, and a primary representation of hybrid rather than fully remote arrangements. Future research should evaluate longitudinal intervention trials, assess applicability in private sector organizations, and test interactions with traditional stress frameworks such as MCPARS.

#### Other relevant information
The authors underscore that traditional leadership competencies remain relevant in digital spaces, but require operational translation to overcome the communication distance inherent in remote work.
