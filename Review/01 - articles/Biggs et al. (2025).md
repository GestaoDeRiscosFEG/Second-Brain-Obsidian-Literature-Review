### Biggs et al. (2025)

#### Reference (ABNT)
BIGGS, Amanda, et al. Managing psychosocial health risks in the Australian construction industry: a holistic hazard management intervention. **Buildings**, v. 15, n. 19, p. 3475, 2025. DOI: 10.3390/buildings15193475.

#### Objective
This case study focuses on a major water infrastructure construction site in regional Australia with a FIFO/DIDO workforce, partnering with MATES in Construction to evaluate an integrated psychosocial hazard management intervention based on the Job Demands-Resources framework, assessing shifts in job demands and resources across project phases and benchmarking mental health outcomes against 11 comparable sites.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Hypothesis 1 predicts that following a holistic psychosocial risk management intervention, perceived job demands (such as role conflict and role overload) will significantly reduce. Hypothesis 2 predicts that perceived job resources (such as supervisor support, job control, praise and recognition) will significantly increase. Hypothesis 3 predicts that workers' psychological distress and suicidal ideation will be lower compared to workers at comparable sites. How the implementation of a holistic, multilevel mental health intervention affects psychosocial risks and psychological wellbeing in remote, high-pressure construction settings.

#### Method
*   **Type:** Longitudinal quantitative case study with repeated cross-sectional survey waves and a comparative benchmark cohort
*   **Application:** Construction workforce operating on the Rookwood Weir regional water infrastructure project in Queensland, Australia, utilizing Fly-In-Fly-Out and Drive-In-Drive-Out (FIFO/DIDO) rosters, benchmarked against 11 equivalent remote sites
*   **Type of work:** Remote, project-based on-site construction work

#### Main findings
Following the implementation of the multilevel intervention, workers experienced statistically significant reductions in job demands between baseline and final evaluation, specifically regarding role overload, role ambiguity, role conflict, and supervisor task conflict. Concurrently, perceived job resources increased significantly, with substantial improvements in job control, coworker support, supervisor support, praise and recognition, procedural justice, and change consultation. Workers at the intervention site exhibited significantly lower levels of psychological distress compared to workers across 11 benchmark FIFO/DIDO sites, though suicidal ideation rates did not differ significantly between groups. Visible leadership engagement and structured third-party partnerships were identified as pivotal facilitators of sustainable risk mitigation.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Physical, Ergonomic, Psychosocial, Cognitive, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** Burnout, psychological distress, suicidal ideation, workload overload, job insecurity, manual labor hazards, remote isolation, and masculine culture stigma
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Psychological distress, emotional exhaustion, burnout, substance abuse, and suicidal ideation

#### Risk management strategies
Establishment of an industry partnership with MATES in Construction to deliver tiered suicide prevention and mental health programs. Execution of organizational audits via The Blueprint for Better Mental Health. Provision of Applied Suicide Intervention Skills Training (ASIST) for cross-departmental site leaders. Delivery of General Awareness Training (GAT) across the workforce and credentialing of volunteer peer Connectors. Routine administration of the People at Work - Construction (PAW-CON) survey to guide targeted corrective actions during operational downtimes. Active modeling of psychological safety and support by frontline supervisors.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[PAW-CON]]: A 36-item construction-specific survey tool underpinned by Job Demands-Resources theory, administered across four project timepoints to measure 11 job demands and resources.
- [[GHQ-12]]: The 12-item General Health Questionnaire, adapted to a 7-point scale to measure psychological distress and minor psychiatric morbidity at Time 4.
- [[SBQ-R]]: Two items adapted from the Suicidal Behaviors Questionnaire-Revised used to assess past and future suicidal ideation.

#### Relation to regulations
The study references the Australian Work Health and Safety statutory framework and the Safe Work Australia Model Code of Practice for Managing Psychosocial Hazards at Work (2022), detailing the four-stage risk management cycle (identification, assessment, control through elimination/minimization, and review).

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Extant research in high-risk industries predominantly relies on fragmented, individual-focused interventions that fail to modify organizational environments or secure sustained leadership buy-in. There is a dearth of structured, multilevel intervention evaluations demonstrating measurable impacts on psychosocial conditions over extended operational timelines. The study notes methodological limitations including independent cross-sectional cohorts rather than matched panel tracking, reliance on self-report data, single-time measurement of suicidal ideation using brief indicators, and the need for economic evaluations and objective safety metrics in future research.

#### Other relevant information
FIFO/DIDO (Fly-In-Fly-Out / Drive-In-Drive-Out) operational model in a remote regional context with environmental disruptors (site flooding, night shifts).
