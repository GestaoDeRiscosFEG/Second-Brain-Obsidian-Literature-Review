### Morton et al. (2024)

#### Reference (ABNT)
MORTON, Stephen, et al. “Are we working (too) comfortably?”: a focus group study to understand sedentary behaviour when working at home and identify intervention strategies. **BMC Public Health**, v. 24, n. 1, art. 1516, p. 1-14, 2024. DOI: 10.1186/s12889-024-18892-1.

#### Objective
To apply the Capability-Opportunity-Motivation-Behaviour (COM-B) framework to investigate occupational sedentary behaviour among desk-based employees working at home, explore line managers' practices and perceptions regarding supporting subordinates to reduce sitting time, and identify promising multilevel intervention strategies.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
How do employees describe their sedentary behaviour when working at home? What factors influence employees' capability, opportunity, and motivation to reduce and break up prolonged sitting? How do line managers describe their support behaviour, and what influences their capability to intervene? What individual and organizational intervention strategies are considered viable by employees and managers?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Qualitative focus group study guided by the COM-B behavioral model
*   **Application:** Six online focus groups involving 42 participants in the United Kingdom (three focus groups with 21 non-managerial desk-based employees aged 18 to 40, and three focus groups with 21 line managers), all working remotely for at least 50% of their working hours
*   **Type of work:** Work from home (remote office work)

#### Main findings
Working at home significantly exacerbated prolonged occupational sedentary behaviour compared to office-based work. The elimination of the daily commute, the loss of incidental movement between office buildings, and the compression of schedules into continuous back-to-back virtual meetings drastically reduced daily physical movement. Powerful social and organizational norms constrained physical activity, notably the expectation of continuous digital availability, where having one's camera turned on is perceived as proof of productive presence. While working at home provided flexible time windows that could theoretically permit exercise, high workload, immersive tasks, and blurred home boundaries often prevented workers from taking breaks. Line managers recognized the importance of modeling healthy movement behaviors, but reported low self-efficacy and limited organizational guidance on how to actively support remote employee movement.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Organizational, Psychosocial
*   **Specific risk factors:** Prolonged uninterrupted sitting, physical inactivity, continuous screen time, constant online availability expectations, back-to-back virtual calls, loss of incidental walking, blurred work-life boundaries, and social isolation
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Elevated risks of musculoskeletal disorders, cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, psychological distress, depressive symptoms, fatigue, and decreased daytime alertness

#### Risk management strategies
Implementation of multilevel behavioral interventions combining individual habits and organizational policy reform. Encouraging standing during virtual meetings and conducting walking phone meetings. Establishing organizational norms that permit turning cameras off to enable stretching and movement during webinars. Active role modeling by line managers (such as publicly blocking calendar breaks and disconnecting on time). Default reduction of meeting durations (e.g., 25 or 50 minutes) to create buffer breaks. Employer provision of height-adjustable sit-stand workstations and movement-tracking software prompts.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[COM-B Model]]: Applied as a theoretical behavioral framework to systematically analyze Capability (physical and psychological), Opportunity (physical and social), and Motivation (reflective and automatic) influencing sedentary behaviour and management support.

#### Relation to regulations
The study references public health guidelines from the World Health Organization (WHO 2020 Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour) and UK Chief Medical Officers, which mandate minimizing extended sedentary time across adult populations.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Empirical intervention research specifically addressing sedentary behaviour in home-working contexts remains in its infancy. Extant workplace health literature primarily examined physical activity in traditional office settings rather than domestic workstations. Future research should implement device-based 24-hour movement measurements (using accelerometers and posture sensors) to capture sitting, physical activity, and sleep patterns accurately. Methodological limitations include potential recruitment bias toward employees already interested in physical activity and a lack of ethnic and socio-demographic diversity in focus group cohorts.

#### Other relevant information
The authors highlight the paradox of working from home: while remote work offers flexibility, comfortable home arrangements and digital surveillance lead employees to sit longer than in formal corporate offices.
