### Ahmed et al. (2022)

#### Reference (ABNT)
AHMED, Shuaib; QAMAR, Faisal; SOOMRO, Suhaib Ahmed. Ergonomic work from home and occupational health problems amid COVID-19. **Human Systems Management**, v. 41, n. 5, p. 535-551, 2022. DOI: 10.3233/HSM-211548.

#### Objective
To examine how the emergency transition to work from home during the COVID-19 pandemic altered workplace ergonomics, to evaluate the layout and suitability of domestic workstations, and to analyze physical discomforts and occupational health problems among university faculty members.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
How well prepared are employees for occupational duties performed at home? Are organizations equipped to fulfill furniture and hardware demands for home offices? Do organizations respect standard working hours during remote work? How do improvised domestic environments affect physical ergonomics and worker health?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Descriptive cross-sectional survey
*   **Application:** Online questionnaire distributed via chain-referral sampling to 480 faculty members across seven higher education institutions in Sindh, Pakistan, yielding 273 complete responses
*   **Type of work:** Remote work (work from home)

#### Main findings
A severe deficit in domestic ergonomic setup was documented: 59% of respondents lacked an adjustable office chair with backrest support, 46.5% improvised with non-standard residential furniture, and over 84% worked online for more than 5 hours daily. Improvised setups generated widespread musculoskeletal discomfort, with 64.1% reporting severe shoulder distress, 58% experiencing neck pain, 54.6% suffering from lumbar backache, and 47.3% reporting lower extremity soreness. Chi-square analyses revealed significant associations between worker age and reported back, shoulder, and wrist pain (p < 0.05). Furthermore, female faculty members reported significantly higher resistance to continuing remote work online after the pandemic compared to males (54.3% vs. 37.7%, p < 0.05).

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Organizational, Physical, Psychosocial
*   **Specific risk factors:** Awkward neck flexion from low laptop screens, lack of forearm support, prolonged static sitting, dining chair use, poor domestic lighting, domestic noise distractions, and blurred boundaries causing extended working hours
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Work-related musculoskeletal disorders, chronic lumbar spine pain, cervicalgia, carpal tunnel strain, tendonitis, eye strain, headaches, and physical exhaustion

#### Risk management strategies
Formulation of institutional checklists and guidance sheets for setting up home workstations. Allocation of dedicated individual employer stipends to subsidize ergonomic chairs, adjustable desks, external keyboards, and mice. Conducting virtual training workshops on postural variation and home ergonomics. Devising realistic institutional policies acknowledging overlapping domestic and professional roles. Consulting occupational therapists and organizational psychologists to manage employee health issues.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Nordic Musculoskeletal Questionnaire (NMQ)]]: Adapted 17-item survey assessing anatomical pain prevalence, postural discomfort, temporal working patterns, and environmental workspace conditions.

#### Relation to regulations
The study references the CSA Z412 office ergonomics standard guidelines (covering display screen equipment, postural requirements, and inventory suitability) and aligns with World Health Organization (WHO) and International Labour Organization (ILO) telework safety directives.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Published empirical research investigating ergonomic hazards among academic and knowledge-worker populations in developing nations remains scarce. The authors emphasize the need for prospective intervention studies testing the Participatory Ergonomics Model. Cross-sectional survey designs and self-report metrics limit objective biomechanical verification of tissue damage. Further research should evaluate multi-sectoral cohorts beyond higher education.

#### Other relevant information
The authors emphasize that reactive interventions following musculoskeletal injury are cost-inefficient, advocating proactive macroergonomic support before workers develop chronic disorders.
