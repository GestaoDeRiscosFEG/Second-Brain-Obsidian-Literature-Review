### Schall and Chen (2022)

#### Reference (ABNT)
SCHALL JR, Mark C.; CHEN, Peter. Evidence-based strategies for improving occupational safety and health among teleworkers during and after the coronavirus pandemic. **Human Factors**, v. 64, n. 8, p. 1404-1411, 2022. DOI: 10.1177/0018720820984583.

#### Objective
To review practical, evidence-based strategies that can be implemented to promote teleworker safety, health, and well-being during and after the coronavirus pandemic of 2019 (COVID-19).

#### Research questions or hypotheses
What practical and evidence-based strategies can management and organizational leadership implement to mitigate occupational safety and health risks among teleworkers during and following the COVID-19 crisis?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Narrative literature review, qualitative
*   **Application:** Synthesis of intervention studies and macroergonomic frameworks applicable to service and knowledge-worker telework populations during pandemic and post-pandemic transitions
*   **Type of work:** Telework (work from home)

#### Main findings
Interventions aimed at increasing worker motivation to engage in safe and healthy behaviors via enhanced safety leadership, managing role boundaries to reduce occupational safety and health risks, and redesigning work to strengthen interpersonal interactions, interdependence, as well as workers' initiation have been supported in the literature. Management role modeling, structured goal-setting, and regular safety communications significantly enhance employee safety compliance and reduce physical strain. Family-supportive supervisor behaviors mitigate work-to-family interference and improve employee physical health and organizational commitment. Work redesign that fosters participatory ergonomics, worker initiative (job crafting), and peer health circles helps counteract social isolation, role ambiguity, and musculoskeletal discomfort.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Ergonomic, Psychosocial, Organizational
*   **Specific risk factors:** Musculoskeletal pain and injuries, social isolation, mental workload, extended working hours, blurred role boundaries, non-neutral postures from crude workstations, role ambiguity, and cybersecurity-induced stress
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Increased incidence of musculoskeletal injuries, accumulation of tissue damage from suboptimal workstations, job stress, physical and mental health problems, and organizational policy conflicts

#### Risk management strategies
Enhanced safety leadership through management role modeling, such as interrupting sitting time with intermittent standing bouts every 30 minutes. Setting specific, challenging, and attainable OSH goals with regular feedback, coaching, and performance monitoring. Active management of role boundaries through clear expectations regarding mobile device use and family-supportive supervisor behaviors. Encouraging teleworker autonomy and job crafting to modify home work arrangements. Redesigning work to incorporate participatory ergonomics, forearm support, mouse-use feedback, and peer support networks such as health circles. Structuring concise, regular OSH-oriented communications prior to team meetings.

#### Tools and techniques
none identified

#### Relation to regulations
The article references institutional guidance issued by the World Health Organization (WHO) and the United States Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) regarding telework infection control and OSH challenges. The authors emphasize internal organizational policies and managerial practices over statutory state legislation.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Compared to working in traditional settings, telework involves OSH oversights arising from the absence of face-to-face supervision, lack of access to relevant ergonomics information and technical support, increased social isolation from colleagues, and blurred boundaries between home and work. From an organizational perspective, rapid restructuring to telework models risks creating policy ambiguities, such as conflicts over document handling and data security protocols on unsecure networks. While standing desks and physical activity equipment provide benefits, further research is required to evaluate their efficacy. Common home office hazards require investigation into practical interventions such as postural variability.

#### Other relevant information
The study emphasizes that because home physical workspaces are not owned by the employer, engineering controls are difficult to mandate, requiring organizations to prioritize leadership development, behavioral motivation, and work redesign.
