### Chen et al. (2024)

#### Reference (ABNT)
CHEN, Y., et al. Pandemic preparedness from the perspective of Occupational Health professionals. **Occupational Medicine**, v. 74, n. 1, p. 93-98, 2024. DOI: 10.1093/occmed/kqad119.

#### Objective
To ascertain how occupational safety and health (OSH) and human resource (HR) professionals in the Republic of Ireland prepared their workplaces prior to the emergence of COVID-19, evaluate organizational readiness levels, and identify management drivers of effective emergency planning.

#### Research questions or hypotheses
How did employers and OSH personnel perceive and respond to the emerging pandemic threat in its early stages? What factors facilitated or hindered timely emergency decision-making across enterprises of varying sizes? How did pre-existing remote work policies and infectious disease contingency plans influence organizational resilience?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Qualitative study incorporating semi-structured online focus groups and embedded quantitative polling
*   **Application:** Fifteen focus groups with 60 OSH and HR practitioners (42 males, 18 females) representing small (10 to 49 employees), medium (50 to 249), and large (250+) enterprises across diverse sectors (construction, manufacturing, aviation, corporate offices) in Ireland
*   **Type of work:** Remote, telework, and essential on-site operations

#### Main findings
The study identified three distinct levels of organizational preparedness: (1) early awareness and preparation; (2) unaware and not ready; and (3) aware, but not ready. While most organizations recognized the severity of the pandemic, they were not fully prepared for operational disruptions. A critical structural divide emerged: multinational subsidiaries with international corporate networks initiated proactive countermeasures early, whereas stand-alone domestic enterprises and small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs) lacked resources, specialized OSH personnel, and emergency protocols, forcing them into reactive crisis management. General emergency preparedness, specifically the pre-existence of formal work-from-home (WFH) policies (present in only 32.2% of surveyed workplaces prior to COVID-19), prior epidemic simulations, and infectious disease contingency procedures, significantly accelerated organizational agility and reduced employee health vulnerabilities.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Organizational, Psychosocial, Physical
*   **Specific risk factors:** Unanticipated operational disruption, absence of telework protocols, deficient home IT infrastructure, management underestimation of airborne biological transmission, reluctance of leadership to prioritize safety over production capacity, and employee mental distress during crisis transitions
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Business continuity failure, elevated workplace infection outbreaks, operational chaos, technological bottlenecks, employee anxiety, and cumulative psychological strain

#### Risk management strategies
Formulating and institutionalizing formal work-from-home policies and remote work infrastructure during non-crisis times. Conducting emergency scenario simulations and updating business continuity plans. Actively involving and empowering OSH professionals in executive decision-making and public health committees. Establishing cross-organizational and international peer learning networks for OSH specialists. Developing holistic risk management protocols that balance immediate physical biosecurity with proactive mental health support for remote workers.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[Zoom Mini-Surveys]]: Quantitative 5-question structured polls embedded within virtual focus groups to triangulate qualitative findings.
- [[NVivo Thematic Coding Framework]]: Qualitative analysis methodology utilizing NVivo software with intercoder reliability testing to synthesize organizational readiness themes.

#### Relation to regulations
The study reviews compliance with European Union Council Directive 2000/54/EC (Biological Agents at Work Directive) and national Irish Health and Safety Authority (HSA) and Health Service Executive (HSE) public health mandates, criticizing public health authorities for initially downplaying workplace airborne transmission dynamics.

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Extant pandemic research predominantly focuses on healthcare facilities and nursing homes, neglecting general occupational settings such as corporate offices, construction sites, and SMEs. While the hierarchy of controls is well documented, there is a scarcity of empirical research examining managerial decision-making processes and leadership motivations that lead to timely safety action. The authors also highlight that participating organizations focused almost exclusively on physical infection control, leaving teleworker mental health and psychosocial support substantially underexplored.

#### Other relevant information
The findings emphasize that workplace emergency planning should not treat OSH as administrative compliance, but rather as an indispensable strategic driver of operational business continuity.
