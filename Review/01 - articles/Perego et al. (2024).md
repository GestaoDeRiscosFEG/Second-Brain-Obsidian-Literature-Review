### Perego et al. (2024)

#### Reference (ABNT)
PEREGO, Francesca, et al. Cardiac autonomic profile, perceived stress and environmental comfort in healthy employees during remote and in-office work. **Scientific Reports**, v. 14, art. 3727, p. 1-13, 2024. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-54268-w.

#### Objective
The primary aim was to determine whether the cardiac autonomic profile (CAP) assessed via heart rate variability (HRV), self-perceived stress, environmental comfort, and workstation comfort differ between remote working days and in-office working days. The secondary aim was to evaluate how these indices correlate with individual and environmental work-related factors (such as cohabiting children, dedicated workrooms, daily work hours, and sleep duration).

#### Research questions or hypotheses
Does alternating between remote work at home and in-office work modify diurnal and nocturnal cardiac autonomic profiles and subjective comfort ratings? How do domestic and environmental variables (e.g., dedicated workspaces, childcare, and overtime) influence cardiac neural regulation and cardiovascular strain?

#### Method
*   **Type:** Repeated-measures within-subject experimental physiological study
*   **Application:** Fifty healthy white-collar office employees (25 men and 25 women; 84% permanent contracts) alternating between remote work at home and in-office work in Italy, monitored using 24-hour electrocardiography and validated psychometric scales between July 2020 and July 2021
*   **Type of work:** Remote and in-office office work (comparative design)

#### Main findings
When evaluating the entire cohort as a single group, overall cardiac autonomic parameters did not differ significantly between remote and office days. However, subjective ratings showed distinct divergence: remote work was characterized by significantly lower perceived stress (5.6 +/- 2.2 vs. 6.4 +/- 1.8, p < 0.05) and higher indoor environmental comfort (7.7 +/- 1.9 vs. 7.0 +/- 1.5, p < 0.05), but significantly poorer workstation ergonomic comfort (6.2 +/- 1.8 vs. 7.5 +/- 1.2, p < 0.05) compared to the corporate office. Crucially, specific work-related factors triggered an adverse physiological shift: teleworkers with cohabiting children, those lacking a dedicated workroom at home, and employees working longer daily hours exhibited significantly decreased nocturnal cardiac vagal modulation (high-frequency HRV power). This autonomic imbalance reflects heightened sympathetic dominance, representing an elevated risk factor for long-term cardiovascular and cardio-metabolic disorders.

#### Identified risks
*   **Classification:** Physical, Organizational, Psychosocial, Ergonomic
*   **Specific risk factors:** Impaired cardiac vagal modulation, long daily working hours, absence of a dedicated workspace, domestic childcare interruptions, suboptimal home workstations (uncomfortable seating, monitor/desk misalignment), and blurred domestic boundaries
*   **Impacts and consequences:** Autonomic nervous system dysregulation, increased long-term risk of cardiovascular disease (hypertension, myocardial infarction), chronic physiological fatigue, and musculoskeletal discomfort

#### Risk management strategies
Designing home workspaces with dedicated, quiet rooms separated from family living areas. Establishing strict limits on daily remote working hours to prevent routine overtime and maintain autonomic recovery during sleep. Provision of adjustable ergonomic workstations (ergonomic chairs and external monitors) for home offices to match corporate standards. Implementation of multidisciplinary occupational monitoring programs involving occupational medicine, cardiology, and psychology to screen remote workers for autonomic strain.

#### Tools and techniques
- [[HRV]]: A 3-lead 24-hour Holter electrocardiogram system (eMotion FAROS, 500 Hz sampling rate) with autoregressive power spectral decomposition, used to compute time- and frequency-domain heart rate variability markers (mean RR, variance of RR, high-frequency absolute and normalized power) during daytime (1 to 4 p.m.) and nighttime (1 to 4 a.m.).
- [[Visual Analogue Scale (VAS)]]: Three 100 mm horizontal analogue scales (0 to 10 points) self-administered by participants to quantify average perceived stress, indoor environmental comfort (lighting, thermal, acoustic, ventilation), and workstation ergonomic comfort.

#### Relation to regulations
The study protocol adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki for medical research involving human subjects and was formally approved by the Ethics Committee of Istituti Clinici Scientifici Maugeri IRCCS (Approval 2467CE).

#### Gaps pointed out by the authors
Previous occupational literature on telework relied heavily on subjective questionnaires, leaving a critical gap regarding objective physiological and autonomic biomarkers. The authors emphasize the need for comprehensive and exhaustive longitudinal evaluations to prevent long-term cardiovascular damage among teleworkers. Limitations include a modest sample size (n = 50), conducting the study during the acute phase of the COVID-19 pandemic, inability to perform stratified sub-analyses by gender, and the necessity to validate environmental comfort using standardized cross-cultural scales (e.g., ASHRAE standards).

#### Other relevant information
The authors note that sleep represents a vital physiological window for cardiovascular recovery; domestic pressures that degrade nighttime vagal tone directly elevate cardiovascular vulnerability.
